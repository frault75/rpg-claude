# PALIMPSEST: Design Document

> *palimpsest (n.): a page of vellum that has been scraped clean and written over. The old words never fully leave.*

---

## 0. At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Title** | *Palimpsest* |
| **Genre** | Story-driven, turn-based RPG with puzzle-like, hand-placed fights |
| **Length** | 2–3 hours: five chapters, a prologue and an epilogue, side stories in every chapter and an optional undercroft |
| **Platforms** | Desktop and mobile browsers, installable as a Progressive Web App and playable offline (§11.1) |
| **Languages** | English and French, switchable at any time (§11.2) |
| **Visual language** | HD-2D: pixel art standing in a lit 3D diorama, with modern light, fog, bloom and depth of field, in the spirit of *Octopath Traveler* and the 16-bit *Final Fantasy* and *Chrono Trigger* games (§8) |
| **Technology** | Static build (Vite, TypeScript, three.js), hosted on GitHub Pages (§13) |
| **Assets** | None. Every pixel, sprite, shader, icon and sound is generated by code |

**Pitch.** In the realm of Hollin, every soul is written in the Book of Names, and for ten years no one has died. People call it the Mercy. Isot, a young scribe sentenced to be scraped out of the Book, escapes with an anchoress who has not left her walled cell in a decade and a knight who has forgotten his own name. Along the way she learns that the Mercy was a crime. The thing the world has been missing since is its ending, and the knight walking beside her is that ending.

**Design pillars**

1. **The world is a book.** Hollin is the text, and the wild places are the margin. Monsters are marginal doodles, and a person who is forgotten is scraped vellum. The game is drawn as HD-2D pixel art, but every rule, sound and story beat belongs to the book.
2. **Plan, don't grind.** Every enemy shows what it will do next. Every fight is hand-placed, visible and fought once, and works as a small puzzle with an exact answer. The party grows with every fight won (§5.16), but there are no random encounters and nothing to farm: experience, pennies and loot are all placed by hand, so the game is balanced for exactly what a player has done. The party is fully restored after each battle. New abilities come from story beats, ranks from levels, and most equipment bends a rule rather than raising a number.
3. **Endings matter.** The story is about letting things finish, so the game itself has to end well and on time. Anything that doesn't serve story, feel or atmosphere gets cut.

## 1. World and lore

### 1.1 Hollin

Hollin is a small, cold, Christian-flavoured realm of fens, villages and one great abbey. The name comes from the holly, which stays green all winter. The abbey of **Saint Ebb's** stands on a tidal island, joined to the mainland by a causeway that the sea covers twice a day. In its church, on a lectern chained to the floor, lies the **Book of Names**.

### 1.2 Cosmology

- **The First Hand** wrote the world on one skin of vellum, the **Great Page**. It wrote the last word first: **FINIS**, at the foot, so that every line above it would have somewhere to run to. Then it wrote the sea, the land, the beasts and the people, each one a line.
- **The Reader** is FINIS given legs. He walks up the page to meet each line at its end and reads it aloud. A line that is read turns to gold and is **bound**: sewn into the spine of the world, where nothing is lost.
- **The Margin** is the border the Hand left for its own jokes, and the first drolleries were its doodles. Whatever is scraped from the text falls into the Margin and takes a doodle's shape. It is not a hell but a waiting room with no door: the Reader reads only what is written.
- **The Ink-Well**, at the bottom of the Margin, is where all scraped ink drains.

### 1.3 The laws of the Book

These are the rules the player needs. They are taught through play, never as a lecture.

1. **What is written is held.** Every soul born in Hollin is written in the Book. Being written means being remembered by the world. This is the first half of the Abbey's motto, carved on the lectern. The full motto is *Quod scriptum est, tenetur; quod lectum est, ligatur*: "What is written is held; what is read is bound." Since the Mercy, the lectern shows only the first half. Raking light reveals the rest in Chapter V.
2. **The last word of the Book is FINIS.** It was written before the first name, because every name ends. FINIS is also the name of the **Reader**, the gentle walker the old songs call Death. When your time comes, the Reader reads your name aloud and you go with him.
3. **A scraped name is forgotten.** Scrape a name off the vellum with a penknife and pumice, and the world forgets that person at once. Only one exception exists: *the hand that scrapes remembers.*
4. **The forgotten go to the Margin.** Within days a scraped person fades out of the text and into the wild ornamental border of the world. There they become *marginalia*: drolleries, grotesques, hybrids. Monsters, in other words.
5. **Without an ending, things fray.** Ten years ago Abbot Aumery scraped FINIS from the Book. Since then no one has died, and they don't heal either. The sick and the very old *fray*. Their colours drain and their outlines break, a little like a painting being un-painted, until they slip into the Margin anyway. The Mercy did not end death. It turned death into forgetting.

### 1.4 History

| Era | When | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| **Ebba's Tide** | ~400 years ago | A storm tears loose the gathering of the Great Page that holds Hollin's souls. Ebba, a fisherwoman, chains it to a tidal rock, and Saint Ebb's grows up around it. |
| **The Scouring of Ninefold** | ~150 years ago | King Oswy the Blotted forges names into the Book and scrapes out his enemies. The Abbey founds the **Pumice Order** to undo the forgeries. The war ends when the Order scours the rebel city of **Ninefold**, nine thousand souls, with **Ebba's Pumice**. |
| **The Grey Year** | 11 years ago | The **Grey Sweat** rises from the Lychford fens. Hild nurses the village and catches it. |
| **The Mercy** | 10 years ago | On the **Night of the Unrung Bell**, Aumery scrapes FINIS as the Reader reaches for Hild. The passing-bell rope goes slack in Hamo's hands. |
| **The Fraying** | Now | The sick and the old drain of colour. Frayed pilgrims walk the roads to a country where death still works. |

### 1.5 Writ: how magic works

**Writ** is the craft of changing the world by changing its text. Its six rules also explain the battle system:

1. **A deed is written before it is done.** Every act in Hollin is inked a breath before it happens. Marginalia are made of ink, so they write their intents openly on banderoles. *(The Omen, §5.2.)*
2. **Ink gives; pumice takes.** Writing adds: wards, glosses and rubrics. Scraping removes. Scraping is easier, and it always leaves underwriting. *(Raking light; Strike Through.)*
3. **Red is read first.** A vermilion rubric gives a word priority and double weight. *(Rubric.)*
4. **Sound reads.** A bell or a voice reads a text aloud and makes it act. A passing bell tells the Reader where to walk, and chant holds wards together. *(Read Aloud, Sermon, the FINIS tolls.)*
5. **Writ costs the writer.** A scribe pays in Ink. An anchorite writes in stone and in her own body, so she pays in blood. The Reader pays in time. *(Ink, Penance, and Whit's Tally and Vigil.)*
6. **The hand that scrapes remembers.**

### 1.6 Factions

- **The Keepers of Saint Ebb's.** The Abbey that holds the Book, led by Abbot Aumery, called the Saint of the Mercy. *Goal:* keep the Mercy.
- **The Pumice Order.** About three hundred grey lay Brothers with pumice maces, led by Prior Gaudry. Under the **Vow of the Light Heart**, each Brother brings the name of someone he lost, and the Abbot scrapes it from his memory. They are serene, tireless and unable to grieve. *Goal:* a page without errors.
- **The Waiting Court.** King Cenwalh has been dying on his throne at Holmcaster for ten years, and his daughter **Eadgyth the Uncrowned** cannot be crowned while he lives. She commands the **Fen Fyrd**, levy archers and eel-spearmen in punts. *Goal:* end the Mercy, for the realm and for her father.
- **The Glossators.** Hedge-scribes who copy names into secret **Underbooks** before they can be scraped. The red manicules that mark interactive objects are their chalk, and the Lost Names are their caches. Wystan was one; Dunstan the sexton still is. *Goal:* that nothing be forgotten.
- **The Court of Unreason.** The Margin's folk: the Hand's doodles, the Mercy's forgotten, and all of Ninefold, 150 years on. They are ruled by the **Abbot of Unreason**, a fox in a mitre who preaches to geese. *Goal:* not to come home, but to be read.
- **Varre**, over the border, has no Book; people die there the old way. The Order holds the **Weir of Varre** against the frayed who walk the **Death Road**.

### 1.7 Key places

- **Saint Ebb's.** Ebba's rock is now the high altar. The causeway floods twice a day, under Ebba's bargain with the sea.
- **Holmcaster.** The royal seat, seen only on the horizon: a court in mourning, waiting for a funeral.
- **Lychford and its Mere.** Where the Sweat began. It has the last passing bell in Hollin, and an old ford lies under the frozen mere.
- **The Blanchwood.** A forest grown over **Ninefold Blank**, a city that survives only in underwriting.
- **Knell Chapel.** Ninefold's chapel to the Reader. It was spared, because no Keeper dared scrape the Reader's house.

### 1.8 Bestiary flavour

Marginalia are forgotten people and old doodles. When beaten, they scatter back into the Margin as sketches.

- **Grylli** are the forgotten who kept only a face. They swarm to fresh scraping like flies to a wound.
- **Hares** are the hunted, now hunting.
- **Snails** are the slowest forgetting. Every marginal knight ends up fighting one.
- **Babewyns** are people scraped mid-quarrel, two heads still arguing.
- **Wodewoses** are forgotten hermits, frayed white.
- **The caladrius** looks away from the dying. With no one dying, it doesn't know where to look.
- **Bishop-fish** bless everything, your enemies included.
- **Ape-scribes** copy nonsense forever.
- **The Blot** is ink left too long without a word.

### 1.9 Legends and songs

- **The Silent Beat.** Every song about the Reader has a rest where his name used to be. Children fill it with a clap: *"Who walks up the page when the candle is low? (clap) comes reading, and gently you go."*
- **Ebba's Bargain.** The sea wanted the Book back. "Twice a day you may try," Ebba said. Twice a day it does.
- **The Bells of Ninefold.** Fishermen hear bells under the fen on still nights. They'll ring "when the last line is read, to call the city home."
- **The Snail and the Knight.** A knight refused to fight a snail: "He's going my way, only slower." The proverb is *Even the snail gets home.* Whit adores it.

### 1.10 Theme

> *"A sentence without a full stop can't be read."* (Isot)

The game is about mercy that refuses endings, about the difference between being forgotten and being finished, and about people who decide for others what those others can bear. Each of the four main characters has made such a decision: Isot, Hild, Aumery and, in his own way, Whit.

**Tone.** The story is tender, eerie and wry. Medieval marginalia are funny (knights fight snails, hares hunt hunters, a head walks around on legs), and the game uses that comedy against the grief instead of to undercut it.

### 1.11 Frame

The whole game is Isot's chronicle. She writes it after the events, over the scraped pages of the Abbey's official *Chronicle of the Mercy*. The interludes between chapters are pages of her chronicle, in her voice and past tense. The game is literally a palimpsest.

## 2. Characters

No archetypes: the party is a **copyist**, a **walled-in recluse** and **Death with amnesia**. Each one has a flaw, a secret and an arc, and each secret has a job in the plot.

### 2.1 ISOT, the Scribe (19). *Role: Editor (control)*

**Who.** A foundling given to Saint Ebb's at five and raised in the scriptorium by old Brother Wystan, the librarian. She has the best hand in the Abbey and knows it. Every morning she brings bread to the anchoress in the church wall, and they argue through the squint.

**Personality.** Precise, quick and wry. She has no patience for sloppiness in letters or in people. She notices everything except her own feelings. Underneath she is tender, and she hides it under corrections.

**Voice.** Short declaratives and corrections, full of words from her trade (quire, descender, gloss, rubric). Dry humour. She never says "I feel".
- *"That's not a monster. That's a gryllus. A head with legs. Lazy illuminators put them anywhere there's space."*
- *"I don't lie. I emend."*
- *"You need a name or you'll fade. I'm writing 'Whit' on your wrist. There's not a whit of you left, and your shield is white. It's efficient."*

**Flaw.** Pride and control. She believes the written word is truer than the lived one, and that a careful enough hand can fix anything. She would rather be right than be known.

**Secret.** **She scraped Brother Wystan out of the Book herself,** the night before the game begins. He was ninety and fraying, losing his words and his outlines, and he begged her to do it. Everything she says in Chapter I about "whoever did this" is a lie.

**Why it matters.**
- Her crime is the hook. She is sentenced to be scraped for it, and the player believes she's innocent.
- The clues are fair. Vellum dust on her knife. Hild's line, "the hand that scrapes remembers", and Isot is the only one who remembers Wystan. A note from Wystan hidden in the underwriting of his psalter.
- Her dark mirror is Sister Ermeline (§2.5), the Abbey's scraper: Isot after three hundred Wystans. Pulling Ermeline out of the Blot is Isot refusing to become her.
- Her confession in the Margin is the emotional peak of Chapter IV.
- In the ending she writes Wystan back in so that he can be properly *finished*: emendation instead of erasure. As Keeper she goes on to write Ninefold back, a street a day.

**Arc.** From scraping to emending, and from hiding mistakes to leaving them visible. She ends as Keeper of the Book.

**Combat role.** She doesn't win fights; she rewrites them. She is fragile and works best from the Rear, spending **Ink** to cancel, redirect and strengthen. A scribe pays in Ink (§1.5).

**Look.** Slight, with cropped dark hair under a linen coif. Azurite-blue habit, fingers stained vermilion and black, a penknife on a cord and an inkhorn at her belt.

### 2.2 HILD, the Anchoress (late 50s). *Role: Warden (protection and positioning)*

**Who.** In the Grey Year, Sister Hild nursed the village of Lychford through the Grey Sweat, caught it, and lay dying in the Abbey infirmary on the Night of the Unrung Bell, the last night anyone in Hollin died. She lived. A month later she had herself walled into an anchorhold, a cell built into the church wall with one small window called the squint, and she hasn't come out since. She is the Abbot's elder sister. She raised him.

**Personality.** Blunt, odd, unsentimental, darkly funny and practical. She looks at people as if she were reading what's underneath them. Ten years alone have made her both very wise and slightly feral.

**Voice.** Short, compressed sentences in plain words with the rhythm of scripture. She never explains herself and answers questions with questions.
- *"Ten years I've watched the world through a hole the size of my hand. It looked smaller then. It was."*
- *"Don't thank me. I've done nothing yet but leave."*
- *"Pain's a bell. After long enough you stop hearing it. That's not the same as it stopping."*

**Flaw.** Self-punishment and withholding. She decides what other people can bear to know. It is her brother's sin, though she would never see it that way.

**Secret.** **She recognises the knight the moment she sees him on the causeway.** He is the Reader. He stood at her bedside ten years ago and vanished mid-reach when her brother scraped his name. She has been dying ever since, slowly and without end. She broke out of her wall to save Isot, but also because she has a plan: get the knight his name back, so that she can finally die.

**Why it matters.** Her silence is the betrayal at the midpoint. Her brother's love for her is the root of the whole Mercy, and hers is the one name his Clean Page would keep (§3.2). Her death, which she chooses and which is gentle and remembered, is the ending.

**Arc.** From walls to an open door. She goes from enduring to letting go, and from deciding for others to asking.

**Combat role.** High HP. Her abilities cost her own HP (*Penance*): an anchorite pays in blood (§1.5). She moves enemies, walls units in, and heals others with her own strength.

**Look.** Tall and gaunt, in undyed grey wool and a lead-white wimple. Grey plague-marks on her cheek and hands. She carries a huge psalter on an iron chain and swings it as a weapon.

### 2.3 WHIT, the Blank Knight (ageless). *Role: Reckoner (timing and damage)*

**Who.** A knight in plain white with an unpainted shield, found standing in the tidal shallows below Saint Ebb's. He has stood there longer than anyone can remember, because nobody can remember him at all. He has no name, no arms and no past. Isot names him.

**Personality.** Courteous, gentle, earnest and literal. Small things delight him like a child: bread, hands, being called by name, the proverb *Even the snail gets home*. He is sad in a way he can't explain. Animals and the dying go quiet around him.

**Voice.** Old-fashioned courtesy. He apologises often and takes idioms literally, and now and then says something terribly profound without noticing.
- *"Forgive me, lady. I don't believe I've ever been introduced to myself."*
- *"I'll stand in front. It seems to be the thing I'm for."*
- *"Whit. Whit. I like it. It's small. I can carry it."*

**Flaw.** He has no will of his own. He does whatever he's asked, protects whoever is nearest, and is terrified of choosing.

**Secret (unknown even to him).** **He is the Reader, Death:** FINIS given legs (§1.2). His name, the last word of the Book, was scraped by Abbot Aumery ten years ago.

**Why it matters.** He is the midpoint twist. He raises the moral question of the second half: should the Mercy end? His first promise (to read the Court of Unreason) and his first choice (to go and get his name) both come in Chapter IV. His choice to remember is the climax, and his reading of Hild's name is the ending.

**Arc.** From a nobody to someone who chooses to be what he is, and to do it gently.

**Combat role.** He hits hard from the Front, sets countdowns that go off later, and keeps vigil so he can strike first. After the twist he can end things outright. The Reader pays in time (§1.5).

**Look.** Tall, in plain mail and plate with a white surcoat and a great helm he never removes until the last scene. He carries a lance with a blank pennon and a blank argent shield. After the midpoint, the word FINIS shows faintly on the shield in gold underwriting.

### 2.4 ABBOT AUMERY, the Saint of the Mercy. *The antagonist*

**Who.** Keeper of the Book and Hild's younger brother; she raised him. Warm, eloquent and fatherly, he genuinely believes he gave the world a gift. Ten years ago, when the Reader came for Hild, he scraped FINIS. He remembers Death, and he recognises Whit at once.

**Power.** He is the greatest living master of Writ (§1.5). He carries **Ebba's Pumice**, the only stone that can scrape the Book's oldest words, FINIS among them. The realm loves him, and the Order would die for him if it still could.

**Motive.** He scraped FINIS to keep his sister. Now the Mercy has rotted into the Fraying, and rather than undo it, he means to *complete* it: the Clean Page (§3.2).

**Voice.**
- *"Child, no one in Hollin has wept at a graveside in ten years. Would you give them back their weeping?"*
- *"You know that weight. Help me carry it."*

**Keeping him sympathetic.**
- He never lies or gloats, and he kneels to those he sentences.
- In Chapter V he offers Isot the pumice and asks her to share the weight.
- Hild names his tragedy: he couldn't bear to be the one left, so he arranged to be the *only* one left.

**Look.** Mitre, white habit, a red cope and a crozier.

## 3. Story

### 3.1 Structure

| Part | Location | Est. time | Set piece | Function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | Title screen, then the Great Page | 2 min | — | Hook: the Book, the Mercy, a knife on vellum |
| **I. The Scraping** | Saint Ebb's by night, and the causeway | 12 min | The Flight from Saint Ebb's | Inciting incident; the party forms |
| **II. The Village Without Graves** | Lychford, Midwinter | 12 min | The Raid on Lychford | The cost of the Mercy; first memory |
| **III. The Blanchwood** | The fading wood, Ninefold Blank and Knell Chapel | 10 min | The Blanching | **Midpoint twist:** Whit is Death |
| **IV. The Margin** | The ornamental border of the world | 11 min | The Fall of Names | Isot's confession; the ink of FINIS |
| **V. The Writing** | Saint Ebb's at dawn | 11 min | The Nave | Hild and Aumery; FINIS rewritten; the ending |

Total is about 58 minutes for a first playthrough, and about 45 for a quick one. Each set piece is one cinematic (§4) plus a one-to-two-minute exploration run, and fits inside its chapter's time.

### 3.2 Aumery's plan: the Clean Page

- **What.** Scrape every name in Hollin at once. With nothing written, nothing frays and no one grieves. The Margin, Aumery believes, will be "a garden without sorrow."
- **The instrument.** **MERCY**, a great bell cast from Hollin's passing bells. Rung over the open Book with Ebba's Pumice laid on the page, each toll scrapes every name within its sound. Nine tolls will cover the realm. He has thirty-nine of the forty bells; the fortieth, Lychford's, he never gets.
- **The price.** The hand that scrapes remembers, so Aumery alone will remember anyone, and he means to carry it all. His Book of Hours sketches the clean Book with one name in it: **HILD**, held forever, while he remembers.

**How the plan escalates**

| Chapter | What the player sees |
|---|---|
| I–II | Bell-carts roll into the Abbey, and Gaudry raids Lychford for the last bell. |
| III | Ermeline scrapes a forest to trap the party. Gaudry: *"Ninefold was a rehearsal."* |
| IV | A test toll pours a hamlet into the Margin, and the gold border bends. One more Scouring will burst it and unbind the Book. |
| V | MERCY tolls at dawn on the Feast of Saint Ebba, and Hollin goes white district by district. |

### 3.3 Prologue

The title screen (§10.6) shows the abbey at night. **New game** plays **C1, The First Word** (§4.2):

- On the Great Page, by candlelight, a hand of light writes FINIS at the foot in glowing gold, then the names of Hollin above it, line by line, from the foot upwards. The cast is among them: Wystan, Hild, Aumery, Gaudry, Ermeline, Eadgyth and Isot herself.
- The Reader, a small white figure, rises out of the word and walks up the page.
- Isot narrates in subtitles:

> *Before the first name, the last word.*
> **I**n Hollin every soul is written, and the writing is kept at Saint Ebb's, where the sea comes twice a day.
> Ten winters ago the Grey Sweat came, and on the worst night of it, it stopped. No one in Hollin has died since. We call it the Mercy.
> I was nine. I gave thanks for it every morning of my life.
> This is the true account of how the Mercy ended, set down in my hand over the old one.

- The camera closes on one line, and a penknife scrapes it away with the rasp of pumice. Bare vellum is left, with the ghost of the writing underneath. The line is Wystan's. The hand holding the knife is never shown, and the person telling the story is Isot: that is the first clue.
- Fade to black, and the card **Chapter I · The Scraping**.

The prologue can be skipped (Escape or B).

### 3.4 Chapter I: The Scraping (Saint Ebb's, night)

**Maps:** Scriptorium, Penitent's Cell, Cloister and its roof, Sea Gate, Causeway.

1. **Scriptorium.** Isot copies by candlelight. The desk opposite hers is empty, its stool pushed in. She talks to it anyway: *"You'd say my descenders are lazy. They are. I'm tired."* A sleepy brother, Cuthwin, asks who she's talking to. *"No one."*
   - **Raking light tutorial** (the core exploration verb, §10.3). Tilting the candle reveals underwriting. On the Book's latest page, the player finds a freshly scraped line with the ghost of *Brother Wystan, librarian* beneath it.
   - **Secret:** Wystan's psalter, still on his desk, holds a note in underwriting: *"Isot. Thank you for the knife. Don't keep my name; keep your hand steady. W."* An alert player can start to suspect Isot here.
2. **Fight 1 (Isot alone).** The Book's margins stir. Two grylli, heads on legs, climb off the page, drawn by the fresh wound in the vellum. This fight teaches intents, the Penknife, Gloss and Strike Through, and each of them pays: one gryllus nips in the open (4 HP, *Nips* · 1); the other hides its hand (*? ? ?*) and gathers itself to charge (5 HP, *in 1* · 4). Measured with a solver over the engine on Normal: the Penknife alone wins with Isot at 3 or 4 HP of 12; striking out the charges, reading the sly one and cutting it in one blow wins at 10. On Illuminated the Penknife alone loses.
3. **The sentence (C2).** Abbot Aumery arrives with two Pumice Brothers. Through the window, bell-carts are being unloaded in the yard. He finds the scraped line, and the brothers find Isot's penknife with vellum dust on its blade. *"Who scrapes shall be scraped. At the dawn bell."* He kneels and is gentle about it, which is terrifying: *"It won't hurt, child. No one will grieve. That is the mercy of it."*
   - **Choice:** *"I didn't do it."* or *(say nothing)*.
4. **Penitent's Cell (C3).** Isot is locked in. Through the squint in the wall, Hild speaks (sample scene in §3.12). Raking light shows the cell wall was once a doorway into the anchorhold. Hild breaks through with her chained psalter, steps out of her cell for the first time in ten years, and joins the party.
5. **Cloister. Fight 2 (Isot and Hild)** against two Pumice Brothers. This fight teaches places, Step, Shove and Shrive. Raking light reveals a door painted over on the cloister wall, the old way down to the Sea Gate.
6. **Set piece: the Flight from Saint Ebb's.** Alarm bells ring, and Prior Gaudry's Brothers hunt the party through the Abbey. The party runs over the cloister roof and down the painted-over stair, and rolls under the Sea Gate portcullis as it drops.
7. **The Sea Gate (C4).** The camera comes down the abbey tower to the sea gate, and the location card reads *Saint Ebb's — The Sea Gate, by night*. Hild: *"Ten years I have not walked further than my cell. My feet have forgotten what stairs are for."* Isot: *"Then no more stairs. Down the causeway, before the tide closes it."* Fog, the tide coming in, torches behind. A knight in white stands motionless in the shallows. *"He's been there since… always, I think. The fishermen row around him."* He says he was waiting for something. *"Is it you?"* **Hild drops her psalter.** She says nothing. He asks to walk with them, and joins.
8. **Boss I: the Great Snail and the Tide** rises out of the sea (§5.14). Whit steps in front of the party, unasked, and fights beside them. The party is complete.
9. **Hook.** Aumery reaches the gate with torches. He sees the knight and goes white: *"Not him. Not* him*."* Then, to Gaudry: *"Fetch me Lychford's bell. It's the last."* The tide closes the causeway between them. On the far shore, Isot names the knight and writes WHIT on his wrist so that he won't fade.

> *Interlude I. Of the Village Without Graves.*
> **W**e crossed with the tide at our heels: a scribe, an anchoress who had not walked in ten years, and a knight who did not know his name. I wrote WHIT on his wrist so that he would not fade, and he thanked me for it every hour until I asked him to stop, and then he thanked me for that.
> I did not tell them what I had done. I am telling it now.

### 3.5 Chapter II: The Village Without Graves (Lychford, Midwinter)

**Maps:** Lychford Lane, the Village, Churchyard and Lych-gate, Bell Tower, Village Green at night, the frozen Mere.

1. **Lane.** Walking, Isot remembers Wystan's lesson: *"Never scrape, Isot. Emend. A small mark above, the true word between. The mistake stays visible. That's the honesty of it."* She unlocks **Emend**. The irony lands later. Then **Fight 3**: three marginal hares, who shoot at the Rear. Afterwards, frayed pilgrims pass on the road, bound for Varre.
2. **The Village.** The story here is told mostly by the environment:
   - Roses still bloom on the lych-gate in the snow. Nothing has died, not even them.
   - The churchyard has no grave newer than ten years.
   - Dunstan the sexton polishes a spade that shines like a mirror. He trusts Isot with a leaf of a Glossator Underbook.
   - Children play "Mercy", a chasing game in which nobody is ever out. Their skipping rhyme is a puzzle clue.
   - The mummers rehearse the play they have performed every night for ten years. *"Every night?" "The Doctor won't let it end."*
   - Some villagers are fraying: pale, their outlines broken.
   - **Goodwife Amabel** is fraying badly. She asks Isot to scrape her. **Choice** (§3.11).
3. **Churchyard.** On the path is an open grave, dug ten years ago and never filled, with its headstone scraped blank. Hild stops beside it: *"They dug me a hole and I built myself a wall instead. I'm good at walls."* Hild unlocks **Immure**.
   - **Secret:** raking light on the stone reveals *HILD OF SAINT EBB'S, WHO NURSED US THROUGH THE GREY SWEAT.*
   - **Fight 4:** two babewyns perched on the lych-gate.
4. **Bell Tower. Puzzle: the passing bell** (§7.2). The passing bell hasn't rung in ten years. Whit is drawn to it. When it finally tolls (C5), the whole village comes out into the snow to listen. Whit staggers and remembers a fragment: *"A bed. A woman with grey on her face. She was afraid. Then she wasn't. I reached for… and then nothing."* Hild turns away. Whit unlocks **Vigil**: *"I kept watch. I remember keeping watch."*
5. **The Green, night. Boss II: the Mummers' Play** (§5.14). The bell has given the play an audience. *"A champion! Step into the ring!"* Isot answers: *"If the play needs an ending, we'll give it one."* Afterwards the paper masks fall away. Under them are fraying villagers, who weep with relief that something, anything, has ended.
6. **Set piece: the Raid on Lychford (C6).** Gaudry's Brothers come for the bell, a river of torches across the snow.
   - The villagers sink the bell through the ice of the mere, and Gaudry's men burn the tower. Hild Immures the door to hold them.
   - The party runs across the cracking mere, following the old ford that raking light shows under the ice.
   - Eadgyth's archers rise from the reeds and cover their escape.
7. **Hook.** Eadgyth: *"He has thirty-nine bells. Whatever he's building, he's one short."* Hild steers the party onward: *"There's a chapel in the Blanchwood where they used to pray to the Reader. If your knight wants his name, he might find it there."* Isot: *"Why are you helping him?"* Hild: *"Because he's lost. I know lost."*

> *Interlude II. Of the Blanchwood.*
> **I**n Lychford the roses on the lych-gate had bloomed for ten winters, and the sexton had polished his spade until it shone like a mirror. When the passing bell spoke, the whole village came out into the snow to listen, the way you listen for a name on the tip of your tongue.

### 3.6 Chapter III: The Blanchwood (midpoint)

**Maps:** Wood's Edge, Ninefold Blank (the Faded Path), Ninefold Gate, Knell Chapel, the Ossuary.

1. **The wood blanches as you go deeper.** Trees are half-drawn, birds are bare outlines, and the music loses notes (§9.3). **Fight 5:** a wodewose, a wild man of the woods, frayed white, with a gryllus.
2. **Ninefold Blank. Puzzle: the Faded Path** (§7.3). The road has faded to blank vellum. Raking light shows the ghost of the old path, which crosses a whole city drawn only in underwriting. Its streets and doorways tell the story of the Scouring.
   - **Secret:** a hermit's ruin holds Lost Names 5–6.
3. **Ninefold Gate.** Prior Gaudry and Sister Ermeline are waiting with the Pumice Brothers: *"The Abbot bids his* sister *come home. And bids the knight be put back where he was."* Isot: *"Sister?"* Hild, shaken: *"I've spent ten years not looking at things. Let me look."* Hild unlocks **Squint**. Then **Fight 6:** Gaudry and two Brothers.
   - After the fight: *"Aumery's my brother. I raised him on bread and psalms. He was a kind boy. Kindness is a dangerous thing in a powerful man."* Isot: *"Anything else you'd like to mention?"* Hild: *"Not yet."*
4. **Set piece: the Blanching (C7).** Gaudry falls back: *"Ninefold was a rehearsal."* On a ridge, Ermeline scrapes the air with a pumice blade, murmuring her litany, and each stroke wipes out a strip of forest. A wave of blank chases the party through the wood to Knell Chapel. Whit lifts Hild and runs.
5. **Knell Chapel. Puzzle: the Danse Macabre mural** (§7.3). In the mural, each living figure is led by a Death. Restoring the mural opens the ossuary and teaches Isot that a correction can point anywhere, which upgrades **Emend**. One painted Death has no face, only a blank shield. *"That one's not finished."* *"Let's go down."*
6. **Ossuary. Boss III: the Danse Macabre** (§5.14). Four skeletons dance in a ring because the one who should lead them never came.
7. **The twist (C8).** The dancers stop, turn to Whit and **bow**. The smallest, a child, takes his hand. Hild uses Squint on his shield. In gold underwriting it reads: **F I N I S.**
   - **Isot:** *"Finis. It's the last word of every book I've ever copied."* **Whit:** *"What does it mean?"* **Isot:** *"The end."* **Whit:** *"…Oh."*
   - **Hild confesses.** She knew him on the causeway; he stood at her bedside ten years ago. Aumery scraped FINIS that night to keep her. *"No one has died since because Death forgot his name. He's been standing in the sea for ten years, waiting for a word."*
   - **Isot:** *"You knew. From the causeway. You let him wonder."* **Choice:** forgive her or don't (§3.11).
   - Without meaning to, Whit says the child's name. Her bones lie down, at peace: the first ending in Hollin in ten years. *"…She was so tired."* Whit unlocks **Read Aloud**.
8. **Hook.** Outside, the world has been scraped to its edge.
   - **Whit:** *"If I remember, people will die. Amabel. Dunstan. One day, you."* **Isot:** *"Yes."* **Whit:** *"Then I don't want to remember."* **Hild:** *"You haven't seen where the forgotten go."*
   - They step off the page.

> *Interlude III. Of the Margin.*
> **E**very book I ever copied ended with the same word. I wrote it ten thousand times and never once looked at it. He had been walking beside us for four days, and he had said thank you for every one of them.

### 3.7 Chapter IV: The Margin

**Maps:** the Edge, the Ivy Road, the Drollery Fair, Wystan's Vine, the Ink-Well.

1. **Off the page (C9).** The party walks out of the scraped wood until the world runs out at the edge of the page. The camera pulls back, and the Margin opens below: giant acanthus and ivy scrolls, bars of burnished gold, and drolleries everywhere. The party steps off the page into it, tiny among the leaves. This is the game's biggest visual moment.
2. **Fight 7** at the Ivy Gate: a caladrius and two hares. This fight teaches Read Aloud.
3. **Puzzle: catchwords** (§7.4). The Margin is made of the borders of thousands of pages, and the exits are labelled with scribes' catchwords.
4. **The Drollery Fair.** The forgotten act out "the world upside down": a hare hunts a hunter, a knight flees a snail, and the Abbot of Unreason, a fox in a mitre, preaches to geese. Half the Court of Unreason is Ninefold, forgotten for 150 years, and the gold border is bending under them. Most of them are gentle and confused, half-remembering their names. They ask Whit: *"Are you the Reader? Is it time?"* If Isot scraped Amabel, she is here as a small hen and remembers Isot. **Fight 8 (optional)** guards a Lost Name.
5. **Wystan's Vine (C10).** An ape in a monk's habit sits at a tiny desk on a vine, copying nonsense (ape-scribes are a classic drollery). *"Isot. Little Isot. Have you brought the knife?"* **Choice:** confess or stay silent (§3.11). Either way, the truth comes out. Isot kneels and lays her penknife on his desk. *"You did a kind thing badly. That's most kindness."*
   - **Isot:** *"I'll write you back. I have ink. I'll write you back and you'll be—"* **Wystan:** *"Old, Isot. In pain. Frayed. No. Write* him *back."* He nods at Whit. *"Then write me after, and let him read me. I'd like to be finished. I was a good long sentence."*
   - Wystan teaches Isot **Rubric**: *"Red is for the words that must be read first."* He tells them that every scraped name drains down to the Ink-Well, and that the first word ever scraped still lies at the bottom.
   - **Whit:** *"Then I'll go and get my name."* This is the first thing he has chosen.
6. **Set piece: the Fall of Names (C11).** A test toll of MERCY pours a hamlet into the Margin as a waterfall of letters. The Fair stampedes. The Abbot of Unreason shoves the party onto a gold bar of the border, and they slide down it as it buckles, towards the Ink-Well, where Ermeline, who has followed them off the page, is already at work. Whit gives the Abbot of Unreason his first promise: to read them all.
7. **Boss IV: the Blot** (§5.14). Ermeline scrapes at the bright word at the bottom of the well, and the ink swallows her.
8. **After the Blot.** One bright word lies in the drained well. Isot fills her inkhorn with it. She pulls Ermeline out of the ink and writes ERMELINE on her wrist, as she once wrote WHIT. Whit holds out his shield: *"When you write it, write it plainly."*
9. **Hook.** Ermeline: *"Nine tolls at dawn. Then nothing."* Every margin borders a book. The party climbs a gold bar up and out through the margin of Wystan's psalter.

> *Interlude IV. Of the Writing.*
> **T**he forgotten did not want to come home. They wanted to be finished. I had scraped a man out of the world and called it kindness; now I carried his ending in my inkhorn, and the Abbey was waking up.

### 3.8 Chapter V: The Writing (Saint Ebb's, dawn)

**Maps:** Scriptorium, Abbot's Lodging, Cloister, Abbey Church (the nave and the high altar).

1. **MERCY (C12).** Dawn on the Feast of Saint Ebba. The party climbs out of Wystan's psalter into the scriptorium, the way the grylli did in Chapter I. MERCY tolls for the first time, and a white ring spreads across the fens. At low tide, Eadgyth's Fen Fyrd storms the causeway. Only Lychford, whose bell is missing from MERCY, stays in colour.
   - The lectern is empty, because Aumery has carried the Book into the church. Raking light on the lectern reveals the scraped second half of the motto: *quod lectum est, ligatur*, "what is read is bound."
2. **Abbot's Lodging.** Aumery's private Book of Hours lies open. Its margins are filled with *Hild*, written hundreds of times, and one leaf sketches the Clean Page: the clean Book with a single name in it.
   - **Secret:** raking light reveals the last entry: *"Forgive me. I could not be the one left."*
3. **Cloister. Fight 9:** Prior Gaudry and two Pumice Brothers, with the Fyrd's arrows arcing over the walls. One of the Brothers hesitates: *"Is it true? Did he scrape the Reader?"* Afterwards Gaudry asks Whit whether his son is waiting, and lowers his hammer. The Brothers step aside.
4. **Set piece: the Nave.** Ermeline opens the church doors for the party, and Hild walls the doors behind them against the Order. MERCY tolls on. Each toll blanks a band of the nave floor, and the party crosses as the tiles vanish, with Ermeline going ahead to open the way. The run ends in C13.
5. **Brother and Sister (C13).** Aumery stands at the Book on the high altar, with Ebba's Pumice in his hand and MERCY above him.
   - **Aumery:** *"Ten years and no mother has buried a child. Not one. You want to give them back their graves."*
   - **Isot:** *"You didn't stop death. You turned it into forgetting. I've seen where they go."*
   - He offers Isot the pumice: *"You know that weight. Help me carry it."*
   - **Hild:** *"You didn't save me, Aumery. You kept me. Like a flower pressed in a book."* **Aumery:** *"I couldn't be the one left."* **Hild:** *"So you arranged to be the only one."*
   - Hild stops punishing herself and gives freely. She unlocks **Benison**.
   - Aumery refuses: *"Then I'll scrape it all. Every name. No one will remember enough to grieve."*
6. **Final battle: Aumery and the Writing of FINIS** (§5.14). Isot writes five letters while the others protect her. After the letter N, Aumery steps into the Book and the battle moves onto its page (§5.15). Each letter tolls a bell and gives Whit back a memory:
   - **F**: *"A man under an apple tree. He asked me to wait until the apples fell. I waited."*
   - **I**: *"A queen, frightened of the dark. I held the candle."*
   - **N**: *"A child who wanted to know if I was cold. I said yes. I was."*
   - **I**: *"Ten thousand beds. Ten thousand hands. None of them were heavy."*
   - **S**: *"A woman with grey on her face. Hild. She was not afraid. Then he scraped my name, and I forgot it."*

   At the letter S, the scene cuts to Lychford, where Dunstan, Hob and the children ring the bell they hauled from the mere. Their toll and the fifth letter are one sound.

### 3.9 Ending (C14)

1. Aumery lunges to scrape the word again. Whit catches his wrist, gently: *"No more, Aumery."* A crack runs down MERCY.
2. **Colour returns.** The blanching lifts, and colour floods back across Hollin. The Margin's overflow rises as lines of gold. For the first time in the game, every pigment appears at once.
3. **Whit, the Reader:** *"Ten years of names. They're all waiting."* **Hild:** *"Read mine first. I've been at the front of the line a long time."*
   - To Aumery: *"Live. Remember me. That's your penance, and it's a kind one."*
   - To Isot (varies with the Chapter III choice): *"Write it all down. The true way. Over the old one."*
   - To Whit: *"Gently, now."*
   - She squeezes Isot's hand, then lets go.
4. Whit reads aloud: *"Hild of Saint Ebb's, who nursed Lychford through the Grey Sweat."* Across the water, Lychford's passing bell rings, an echo of Chapter II. Hild's figure does not blank out. **It turns to gold, line by line,** and she is gone. Being finished looks different from being forgotten.
5. Isot writes Wystan's name back into the Book in a plain hand. Whit reads it. Far off in the Margin, a small ape-scribe sets down his pen and becomes a line of gold. Amabel gets her ending too, as described in §3.11. Gaudry hears his son's name read. At Holmcaster, King Cenwalh is read in his sleep.
6. Whit removes his helm. We never see his face, only Isot's reaction. *"Oh. You're…"* *"Plain?"* *"Kind."* He asks her to keep the Book: *"Write them, so I can read them."* *"I'll read yours too, one day. Not soon. And slowly."* He walks out along the causeway at low tide.
7. The day after the first funeral, Eadgyth is crowned: the first coronation in Hollin in ten years.

### 3.10 Epilogue

Aumery asks to be walled into Hild's empty anchorhold, and is. Isot, now Keeper of the Book, writes Ninefold back into the Book a street a day, from Ermeline's litany and the Glossators' Underbooks. The epilogue is the last page of her chronicle:

> *Epilogue. Explicit.*
> **H**ere ends the Book of the Mercy.
> I keep the Book of Names now. I write every name in a plain hand, and I leave room at the end of each line.
> My brothers ask why I have drawn three small figures in the margin of this last page: a scribe, an anchoress, and a knight with a white shield. I tell them the margin is where we keep what matters and does not fit.
> Some evenings, when the tide is out, a white figure walks the causeway with a long road of beds before him. He waves. I wave back. He is in no hurry for mine, and I have asked him to read it slowly.
> **FINIS**

The final page's margin lists every **Lost Name** the player found, written in red (§3.13). Then the credits.

### 3.11 Light branching

| When | Choice | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| Ch I, the sentence | Deny it, or stay silent | Aumery's reply. Hild's remark after the confession in Ch IV: *"You lied to an abbot with a straight face. I was impressed."* or *"You didn't even lie. That's how I knew."* |
| Ch II, Amabel | **Scrape her**, refuse, or promise to come back | **If scraped:** she vanishes; Hob lays the table for one and can't say why; Hild says quietly, *"You've done that before."* (foreshadowing); Amabel appears as a hen in the Margin and is written back and read in the ending. **If refused or promised:** she keeps fraying, and in the ending she is read at home with Hob's hand in hers. If Isot promised, Isot is there too. |
| Ch III, after the twist | Forgive Hild, or don't (*"You decided for him. Like your brother."*) | Hild's last words to Isot. |
| Ch IV, Wystan | Confess, or let Wystan say it | Who tells the party, and Whit's line in the ending (*"You told them yourself. That's the harder kind of writing."*). |

### 3.12 Sample scene: the squint (Ch I)

> **HILD:** You're late. And you didn't bring bread.
> **ISOT:** I've been sentenced to be scraped at dawn.
> **HILD:** That's no excuse for no bread.
> **HILD:** Did you do it?
> **ISOT:** No.
> **HILD:** Hm. The hand that scrapes remembers, they say. Who do you remember, Isot?
> **ISOT:** …Brother Wystan. The librarian. Seventy years at that desk.
> **HILD:** Never heard of him.
> **ISOT:** No one has. That's the point.
> **HILD:** You read scraped pages by tilting the candle. Read the wall.
> *(Raking light: a bricked-up doorway shows beneath the plaster.)*
> **HILD:** Stand back. Ten years I've looked at the world through a hole the size of my hand.
> *(The wall comes down: C3.)*
> **HILD:** It looked smaller then. It was.

### 3.13 Lost Names (optional collectible)

Ten names are hidden in underwriting across the five locations, two in each. They are Glossator caches (§1.6). Each one is a single line. In the ending, Whit reads the ones the player found, and the margin of the final page lists them in red.

1. *Edda Thatcher, who mended every roof in Lychford and was afraid of ladders.*
2. *Brother Osric, who sang a quarter-tone flat for forty years and was loved anyway.*
3. *Wat the ferryman, who never once charged a widow.*
4. *Little Joan, who gave a name to every hen in Hollin.*
5. *Maud of the mill, whose laugh sounded like a door that wants oil.*
6. *Gervase, a pedlar of ribbons, who walked every road twice.*
7. *Old Cutha, who kept bees and told them everything.*
8. *Nell and Tom Fisher, married sixty years, who argued about the same boat for all of them.*
9. *Hamo the bellringer, who rang the passing bell for the last time on the night of the Mercy.*
10. *A girl of Lychford, born in the Grey Year, who was never written in time.* (Isot gives her a name in the margin.)

Where they lie: Little Joan in the penitent's cell and Brother Osric on the cloister wall (Saint Ebb's); Edda Thatcher on her own house and Hamo in the ringing chamber (Lychford); Maud and Gervase at the hermit's hearth (Ninefold Blank); Cutha and the Fishers in the Margin; Wat on the cloister wall at dawn, by the church door, and the girl of Lychford on the infirmary roll in the Abbot's lodging (Chapter V). Reading one shows a card and adds it to the save.

### 3.14 Side stories and returned names

**Returning a name.** A Lost Name, once read, can be returned to someone who lost it. They remember, grieve, and are glad. Each return is a short scene and gives experience and a keepsake:
- **Brother Osric** goes to Brother Cuthwin in the dawn scriptorium (Ch V). He sang beside Osric for thirty years and hums the flat note without knowing why.
- **Edda Thatcher** goes to her widower Ralf at the Fen Mill (Ch II), who mends roofs he can't remember learning to mend and is afraid of his own ladder. Her name tells him why: the fear was hers, and he held the ladder for her for thirty years. He comes down without looking, and gives Isot a twist of her straw.
- **Hamo the bellringer** goes to Dunstan (Ch II), who climbs the tower at the sound of the passing bell and writes him into his Underbook: *HAMO, WHO RANG*. He gives Isot a knot of the bell-rope.
- Each return is worth experience once (12 to 18), and the Journal keeps the keepsake under the name.
- **Maud of the mill** goes to the hermit at the Charcoal Hollow (Ch III), who was her brother and has kept the kilns lit for a hundred and fifty years so she would see the fire from the white. *She isn't coming, is she.* He sits down for the first time, and gives Isot willow charcoal: *Draw her a door that doesn't want oil.*
- **Gervase** goes to himself (§6.3).
- **Old Cutha** goes to the bees (Ch IV). Behind the Fair there is a skep that has had no one to talk to, and Isot *tells the bees*, as the old custom is: they come out slowly, *the way people come out of a church*, and leave a comb of honey on the bench.

**Optional places**, one or more in every chapter, each with its own fights, caches and new marginalia:
- **Ch II, the Fen Mill.** Lychford's mill on the frozen fen, whose wheel has turned under the ice for ten years. Corpse-candles burn there, people who should have died and now light the fen (S1); when they go out, Whit says they looked at him *as if I were late*. Ralf is found on its roof. It is reached from the lane, through the gateway in the hedge, once the hares are dealt with.
- **Ch III, the Charcoal Hollow.** A clearing in the Blanchwood where the burners' kilns still smoulder, a hundred and fifty years after the burners were scraped; near the fires the wood keeps its colour. Ember-grylli and a wodewose mother live there (S2): she only keeps them warm. The hermit of Ninefold rakes the kilns. It is reached down a thread of smoke south of the path, once the wild man of the wood is past, and Ninefold now has a way back west to it.
- **Ch IV, the Fair's back lanes.** Reached between two stalls at the bottom of the Fair. The ape-scribes' copying stall (S3: marginalia that copy the last thing done to them), the skep of bees, and a hidden cache. Gervase keeps his stall in the Fair itself.
- **Ch V, the Undercroft.** The Rasure Vault under the scriptorium, where ten years of scrapings have been swept down a chute. Gervase, at his last stall by the third window of the scriptorium at dawn, leans on what Isot always took for a cupboard: a low round-headed door, older than the wall. Down there MERCY is only a hum in the stone.
  - **The sweepers (S4).** A Brother scraped so long ago his robe is the colour of vellum sweeps the shavings from one side of the vault to the other, with three inkhorn hounds at his feet. The raking light finds his name by the chute: *GODRIC RASOR, XL ANNOS*, the rasor who kept the pumice and scraped the skins for forty years. Given his name back in the margin, he leaves the fight with his hounds, tells who he was, and goes up to see the scriptorium by daylight for the first time. Felled, he comes apart into the drift and his broom stands a moment alone.
  - **The palimpsest knights (S5).** Two knights written over something older keep the inner bays. Once they are scraped off, the wall behind them reads *DOMINE, FAC ME UTILEM*: Lord, make me useful.
  - **The Heap (B6).** Every letter ever scraped in Saint Ebb's, trying to become a word. It is **ADSUM**, *here*, the answer at the roll-call: everyone it was made of, answering a roll nobody read. Whit reads it; Isot kneels in the shavings and reads the names on them until her candle is half gone, and at each one a curl of vellum lies down flat, *the way a dog lies down when it is home*. At the bottom is the slip of the roll-call (§6.4).

## 4. Cinematics

### 4.1 How cinematics are made

Fourteen cinematics carry the story's turns. They are played in engine, in the same diorama, with the same pixel art, lights and particles as play; there is no pre-rendered video. A cinematic is a story script (§13.3) that adds camera and screen steps to the usual dialogue, run by the **director** (§13.2). Each step returns a promise, so a cutscene reads as a plain sequence of waits:

- camera pans and zooms, eased, that can follow an actor; slow push-ins and pull-backs; focus pulls with the depth of field;
- letterbox bars, location cards and subtitles (§10.5);
- fades and flashes in the post chain (§8.3);
- character moves and emotes;
- dialogue, with portraits in their moods;
- screen shake.

Shake and flashes follow the accessibility settings (§10.8). Each set piece is one cinematic plus a short exploration run. Each entry below gives the camera, the staging, the effects, the music and the key lines.

## 5. Combat

### 5.1 The battle screen

A battle is fought where it is met, on a stage in the diorama (§8.2), lit by the same moon, lamps and fog as the map. The layout follows *Final Fantasy* and *Octopath Traveler*: **enemies on the left, the party on the right**, facing each other across the stage. A gothic arcade may stand behind as set dressing, from the location's palette.

- **Enemies** stand in up to four places, numbered from the front (**1st–4th**; the 1st is nearest the party). Large bosses take two.
- **The party** stands in three places: **Front** (nearest the enemies), **Middle** and **Rear**. The default formation is Whit in the Front, Hild in the Middle and Isot in the Rear. The player sets it in the pause menu's Party page (§10.7). In a fight with fewer allies, they fill the places from the Front.
- **Intents.** Above each enemy floats a **banderole**, the speech scroll of medieval art. Its intent for the round is written on it. First, in red, the deed alone, in a few words (*Looses an arrow*, *Bites*, a Mummer's couplet). Under it, in small brown italic, goes anything else it does (*Strips Ward*, *Smudge*, *in 1*). What the blow deals sits at the scroll's right end, a small drawn sword and the number, in the same place on every scroll. Whom it strikes is not written: it is marked on the field, as the eye expects. The aim is worked out from where the blow really falls, so an Emended blow shows its new target, and a Step moves the marks at once. A hand-to-hand blow from an enemy behind the first rank marks no one and reads *Out of reach*: it lands only if those in front of it fall first. Each scroll is as long as its words need. Each banderole is numbered with its order of action. Over each ally about to be struck float the roundels of the deeds that will strike them and the sum they will take (*① ② ③ −12*); a deed that tends to another enemy (a heal, a guard, a raising) puts its roundel on that enemy's plate. Pointing at a banderole draws a dotted red line to whom it touches, and the help line says it in words (*Looses an arrow — Isot, 4 damage*). The banderoles find room around the plates and these marks.
- **Commands** sit in lapis windows in the menus' style (§8.8): the chosen ally's abilities with their costs, HP as a bar and a number, and Ink as small inkpots.
- **Defeated marginalia** fray to outlines and scatter back into the Margin (§8.6).

The battle animation speed (normal or fast) is a setting (§10.8).

### 5.2 The round

1. **Omen.** Every enemy writes its intent on its banderole. Some intents take effect the moment they are declared (§5.3).
2. **Party phase.** Each standing character acts once, in any order.
   - Actions resolve immediately, so you see the result before choosing the next one.
   - Once per round, a free **Step** swaps two neighbouring allies.
   - **Undo** works freely within this phase. It is a planning game, not a dexterity test.
   - **End turn** closes the phase, whether or not everyone has acted.
   - An action that isn't allowed is refused, and the screen says why: not enough Ink, not enough HP to pay, must stand in the Front or Middle, out of reach, too strong to Read, already acted, immured, the target can't be targeted, not a single blow (for Emend), already used this battle.
3. **Enemy phase.** Intents resolve in their numbered order, against whoever stands where they point at that moment.
4. **Round's end.** Countdowns tick (Tally, wind-ups), Immure ends, Rubric and doom fade, Ink refills by 1, and the next Omen begins.

### 5.3 Reading intents

- **Aimed at a place** ("the Front"): hits whoever stands there when it resolves. If you move, someone else takes it.
- **Aimed at a person** ("Isot"): follows her wherever she stands.
- **Close intents** need the enemy to stand in the 1st or 2nd place, and can strike only the Front or Middle. Push the enemy further back and the intent fizzles ("cannot reach").
- **Empty or fallen target:** if the target place holds a fallen ally or nobody, the blow carries back to the next standing ally behind it, or forward if there is none. A Close blow that would carry back past the Middle fizzles.
- **Immured units** can't be aimed at. Intents against them fizzle, and a blow aimed at an immured ally's place does not carry.
- **Wind-ups** show a countdown ("in 1"). A winding-up enemy holds its banderole and plans nothing else; the blow lands at its enemy phase once the count reaches 0. Strike Through cancels a wind-up outright.
- **Declared at the Omen.** Withdrawing into a shell makes the enemy Shelled at once, through your phase too; coming out of it takes Shelled away at once; a caladrius's doom marks its target at once. Striking Through a withdrawal keeps the enemy out of its shell.
- **Hidden intents** show **?** until they are Glossed or Squinted.

### 5.4 Resources

- **HP.** Numbers are small so the arithmetic stays in your head: Isot 12, Hild 22, Whit 18.
- **Ink (Isot).** Starts at 2, refills 1 per round, maximum 3.
- **Penance (Hild).** Some of her abilities cost her own HP. She can't pay with her last point.
- **Whit** has no resource. His power is in timing.

### 5.7 Positioning, timing and synergy

- **Positioning.** Place-aimed blows can be dodged with Step. Close attacks fizzle if Shove pushes the attacker back. Lance and Shove need the right places on both sides. Some bosses move your party around.
- **Timing.** Tally counts down to a Reckoning that you can aim at a moment of vulnerability. Immure delays an intent rather than cancelling it. Wind-ups give you a deadline. The order of actions within your phase matters: *act first, then Immure* protects an ally who has already used their turn.
- **Synergies, by design:**
  - Gloss followed by any hit: +3 damage.
  - Isot's cheap Penknife hits wind down Whit's Tally.
  - Shove brings an enemy into Lance range, or pushes a Close attacker out of reach.
  - Emend sends a blow onto a Warded Hild, or, after the upgrade, into another enemy.
  - Strike Through a withdrawal, and a snail stays out of its shell until it next withdraws.
  - Rubric doubles a partner's key move, most dramatically Rubric with Read Aloud (Boss IV).
  - Vigil plus Emend: redirect an attack onto Whit's watch.

### 5.9 Winning, losing, retrying

- **Victory:** every enemy has fallen, or the fight's objective is met (Boss V). Defeated marginalia aren't killed, because nothing in Hollin dies. They scatter back into the Margin as sketches.
- **Defeat:** every ally has fallen, or the objective has failed. *"The ink runs. Every fight is the same puzzle. Try another way."* You can **Try again**, which restarts the same fight instantly, or **Return to the page**, which goes back to the map.
- **No attrition.** The party is fully restored after every battle, so each fight is a self-contained puzzle.
- **Easier, never forced.** After two defeats in a row, *Try again · one step easier* joins the choices (Illuminated to Normal, Normal to Story, §5.17).

### 5.10 Determinism

There are **no hidden rolls**: no misses, no critical hits and no damage ranges. Enemy behaviour is scripted as patterns and conditions. If an enemy has to choose between equal options, it uses a random generator seeded per encounter, and the choice is shown on its banderole before you act. Retrying a fight gives you the same puzzle.

### 5.13 Boss design principle

Every boss is a puzzle with **more than one solution**, and each one leans on a *different* pair of abilities:

| Boss | Main test | Key pairing |
|---|---|---|
| Great Snail | Timing | Whit's Tally with Isot's Strike Through |
| Mummers | Positioning and kill order | Hild's Shove with Whit's Lance and Tally |
| Danse Macabre | Hidden information and redirection | Hild's Squint with Isot's Emend |
| The Blot | Restraint and resources | Isot's Rubric with Whit's Read Aloud |
| Aumery | Protection and action order | All three: Immure after Inscribe, Emend, Benison |

### 5.14 Bosses

#### Boss I: The Great Snail of the Causeway
*In English and French marginalia, knights fighting snails is the oldest joke there is. Here the joke has teeth.*

- **HP 24**, drawn large across the 1st and 2nd places. It repeats a three-round cycle:
  1. **Horns out:** *Lashes the Front · 5* (Close). It comes out of its shell as it declares this, so it is vulnerable all round.
  2. **Withdraws into its shell:** Shelled from the Omen, through your phase, and it *licks its wounds: heals 4* as the intent resolves. A second banderole warns: *Gathers the tide… (next: everyone · 3)*.
  3. **Slime tide:** *Drenches everyone · 3*. It is still Shelled.
- **The Tide** is an environment banderole at the edge of the field: *a wave breaks over the Front · 2 every third round.*
- **Solutions.**
  - Time Whit's Tally so it reaches 0 during a *Horns out* round. For example, set it in round 3, last; the round's end takes it to 2, and two hits in round 4 trigger a full 7-damage Reckoning.
  - Strike Through the withdrawal. The snail stays out of its shell until it next withdraws, three rounds later.
  - Strike Through the slime tide.
  - Step Hild into the Front before the lash.
- **Teaches** that intents are promises and that countdowns can be aimed. Measured with a solver over the engine: a player who only attacks the front still wins, but in about eight rounds and badly hurt (the shell caps each hit at 1 and the snail heals in it); a Tally timed for the horns wins in five; Striking Through the withdrawal wins in four with the party barely touched.

#### Boss II: The Mummers' Play
*Lychford's players have performed the same Midwinter play every night for ten years. In the play the Doctor raises the slain, and in a world without death, he really can.*

Each intent is shown as a rhyming couplet with the rule written underneath.

| Mummer | Place | HP | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Saint George** | 1st | 12 | *"Here comes I, Saint George, the valiant man; I'll smite the foremost if I can!"* (**Front · 4**), alternating with *Stands guard over the Doctor* (the Doctor can't be targeted this round) |
| **Bold Slasher** | 2nd | 10 | *Slashes the Middle · 3* (Close), alternating with *Hurls his blade at the Rear · 3* (Far) |
| **Doctor Ball** | 3rd | 8 | If a mummer has fallen: *"I've a little bottle by my side; the fellow's up who should have died!"* (**raises one at full HP, once per round**). Otherwise: *Doses George: heals 4* |

- **Solutions.**
  - Shove rotates the line. One Shove brings the Doctor within Lance range, but only on rounds when George isn't guarding him.
  - Set Tally on the Doctor. The count ignores guard and reach.
  - Strike Through a revival.
  - Fell George and the Slasher in the same round. The Doctor can raise only one.
  - Use Vigil to punish George's smite.
- **Ending.** The paper masks fall, and frayed villagers weep with relief that something has ended.

#### Boss III: The Danse Macabre
*Four skeletons in the rags of their stations (a Pope, a King, a Ploughman and a Child) dance in a ring, because the one who should lead them never came.*

- **Hidden intents.** Every banderole shows **?** until it is Glossed (one) or Squinted (all, plus the next round).
- **Only the Leader can be harmed** by party attacks. The others are *Hollow*, and blows pass through them. The Leader is the Child, and nothing reveals it except a Gloss or a Squint.
- **The dance turns.** At every round's end the dancers rotate one place. A banderole at the top shows which way.
- **Followers' intents:**
  - *Takes the hand of the Front:* Front · 3, then swaps the Front ally with the Middle ally. The dance pulls your formation apart.
  - *Whirls:* Middle and Rear · 2.
- **The Leader's intent:** *Calls the tune: every dancer acts twice next round.* Strike Through it, or Immure the Leader.
- **Dancers can hurt each other.** An upgraded Emend can turn a follower's blow onto the Leader. The Leader has **24 HP**.
- **Solution.** Find the Leader, then track it through the rotation. Tally follows it wherever it goes, Emend turns the dance against itself, and Immure stops a dancer from scrambling your places.
- **Ending.** The dancers bow to Whit, which leads into the midpoint twist (§3.6).

#### Boss IV: The Blot
*Every scraped name has drained down to the Ink-Well for ten years, and the pool has woken up. Somewhere inside it is the first word ever scraped.*

- **The Blot:** **30 HP**, in the 2nd place. Any *single hit of 4 or more* splits off a **Blotlet** (4 HP) into the nearest empty enemy place.
- **Blotlets:**
  - *Seeps back into the Blot: heals it 5* (only when next to it).
  - Otherwise, *Spatters the Rear · 2*.
  - Hitting one with Isot's Penknife **fills her pen: +1 Ink**.
- **The Blot's intents:**
  - *Engulfs the Front · 5 and Smudges* (the Smudge lands only on Isot).
  - *Swallows a name: heals 6.*
  - *Wells up: a Blotlet rises.*
- **Ink does not die.** Damage can't bring the Blot below 1 HP. It must be **Read**.
- **Solution.**
  - Big hits are a trap, because they make Blotlets.
  - Use small hits, harvest Blotlets for Ink, Read them, and Immure any that are about to seep back.
  - Once the Blot is at 12 HP or less, **Rubric then Read Aloud** (threshold doubled to 12) ends it.
- **Ending.** In the drained well lies one bright word, FINIS. Isot fills her inkhorn with it.

#### Boss V: Abbot Aumery and the Writing of FINIS
*Saint Ebb's at dawn. The Book lies open on the altar. The lectern stands at the party's Rear. Aumery has the Abbey behind him and pumice in his hand.*

- **Objective.** Isot, at the lectern in the Rear, uses **Inscribe** to write **F‑I‑N‑I‑S**, one letter per action.
  - If Isot takes any damage in a round in which she inscribed, that letter smudges and is lost.
  - If Isot falls, the battle is lost.
- **Aumery: 40 HP.** He can't be defeated by damage, but every 10 damage makes him **Falter**, which cancels his next intent. This keeps the party's attacks meaningful.
- **Aumery's intents:**
  - *EDICT: let none stand before me:* at round's end, Front · 6.
  - *Scrapes WHIT from the page:* Whit is **Forgotten** for 2 rounds.
  - *Pumices the page:* **Isot** · 4. This is aimed at her by name, so Step won't save her.
  - *Sermon:* Ward 6.
  - *Calls a Brother:* a Pumice Brother enters an empty place.
  - After the 4th letter: *Gathers the pumice… (next round: every letter scraped).* Strike Through it, Immure him, or make him Falter.
- **Support:** two Pumice Brothers, in the 1st and 3rd places, 9 HP each.
- **Tools.**
  - **Inscribe first, then Immure Isot.** She can't be targeted, and her letter stands.
  - Emend can move *Pumices the page* onto Hild.
  - Benison's Ward soaks a spatter.
  - Tally plus Lance makes Aumery Falter at the right moment.
- **Sound.** Each letter tolls a bell. The fifth toll merges with the passing bell of Lychford.

#### Boss VI (optional): the Heap
*The far end of the Undercroft. Every drift of shavings rises into one heap, higher than a man, moving like a page in a draught.*

- **Objective.** The Heap is trying to say a word, **ADSUM**. It can only be Read, and only once the word is whole; then it can be Read at any strength. The word shows above the field as dots, a letter taking the place of each dot as it is found.
- **The Heap: 30 HP, Read only, two places.** **Every blow from the party knocks a letter loose** (the first time, *Knocked Loose: it was trying to say something*), so the party mostly leaves it alone.
- **Its intents, in turn:**
  - *Reaches for its next letter: thrashes the Front · 4.* If it reaches, the word has one more letter, whether or not the blow lands.
  - *Sheds a stray letter:* a Stray letter rises into an empty place. **Cut down, it falls back into the Heap,** a letter nearer.
  - *Reaches for its next letter: rakes the Middle and the Rear · 3.*
  - *Mouths a word it learned upstairs: F, I, N, I, S… everyone · 5 (in 1).* Not its word: Strike it Through, or Immure the Heap.
- **Tools.** Lance and Penknife on the strays (Shove, Tally and Vigil would strike the Heap); Strike Through for the false word; Shrive, Benison and wax seals to stand the reaching; Read Aloud at the end.
- **After.** Whit reads ADSUM. The slip of the roll-call is left at the bottom of the Heap: the wearer can't be Forgotten.

### 5.16 Growth: levels, experience and ranks

The Book remembers the three of them together, so **the party shares one level**, from 1 to 12.

- **Experience** comes from every fight, once, at a fixed amount shown on the victory scroll, and from side stories and returned names (§3.14). Nothing can be fought twice, so there is nothing to farm: the main path reaches level 9 by the last fight, and everything optional together reaches 12. The last fight gives nothing, because nothing comes after it.
- **Each level** raises the party's HP. Hild and Whit gain about one point per level and Isot one every other level, from 12/19/15 at level 1. At level 3 they are 12/22/18, the numbers the first fights were tuned for.
- **Ranks.** Some levels sharpen an ability. The victory scroll announces each one with its new rule.

| Level | Rank |
|---|---|
| 4 | **Shove II**: 3 damage |
| 6 | **Lance II**: 5 damage |
| 8 | **Penknife II**: 3 damage |
| 10 | **Shrive II**: heals 8 |
| 12 | **Ink-well**: Isot's Ink holds 4 |

- **The victory scroll** shows the experience and pennies gained and fills the level bar from the old total to the new. A new level rings in with its HP and its rank.

Fights are tuned for the level the main path is sure to give, with every optional fight (F8) skipped, and the tests play each one at that level (§13.4). Optional content makes the main path a little easier, and that ease is its reward.

### 5.17 Difficulty

Three modes are offered at New Game, and the mode can be changed at any time in the settings:

| Mode | Party | Enemies |
|---|---|---|
| **Story** (*Conte*) | +50% HP; Ink refills by 2 | every blow deals 1 less (never below 1) |
| **Normal** | as tuned | as tuned |
| **Illuminated** (*Enluminé*) | as tuned | +25% HP (rounded up); blows of 3 or more deal 1 more |

Banderoles always name the blow as it will land in the chosen mode, warnings of a blow to come included.

The tests play every fight in every mode, at the level the story gives:
- in Story, plain fighting wins every ordinary fight and the first two bosses;
- in Normal, plain fighting wins every ordinary fight, the Snail is a slog without its answer, and the Danse Macabre and the Blot can't be won without reading them (§5.12);
- in Illuminated, plain fighting loses the very first fight and the Snail;
- in every mode, every fight is won by its answer. A beam search over the engine finds it, and the last fight's is written out.

## 6. Equipment

Equipment never inflates stats. Every item is a sidegrade that bends one rule, so equipping is choosing how to play.

- **Slots.** Each character has a **relic** slot, for an item that is theirs alone, and a **charm** slot, which anyone can fill. A charm is worn by one character at a time.
- **Finding.** Items are found at story beats and in Glossator caches, or bought from Gervase the pedlar (§6.3); none can be farmed. Once had, an item is carried for the rest of the game.
- **Equipping** happens in the pause menu's Equipment page (§10.7): each slot opens a list of the items that fit it, with their rule and a line of lore. Taking a charm worn by someone else moves it.
- An item only works while its wearer is in the fight.

### 6.3 Gervase, the pedlar of ribbons

*"Gervase, a pedlar of ribbons, who walked every road twice"* is one of the Lost Names (§3.13): a man of Ninefold, scraped a hundred and fifty years ago, who never stopped walking. He sells from a pack of faded ribbons and does not know his own name.

- **Where he is found:**
  - on Lychford Lane (Ch II);
  - at the Wood's Edge (Ch III);
  - at the Drollery Fair (Ch IV);
  - at the mouth of the Undercroft (Ch V), whose way he knows, because he has walked every road twice. His last stall: nothing new, everything still unsold. He shows the party the low door, and, if his name has been read, says that after this he will only walk the one road.
- **His stock** grows from stall to stall, and nothing the party owns is offered again:
  - Lychford Lane: poultices, wax seals, oak-gall ink; the hare's-foot brush (30d), the rosary of oak-galls (26d), the coronel (32d);
  - the Wood's Edge adds holy water; the silverpoint (38d), the leper's clapper (34d), Saint Ebb's girdle (36d);
  - the Drollery Fair adds sal volatile; the horn inkwell (44d), the plumb-line (40d), the pilgrim's scallop (42d).
- **On the lane** he is knotting the tenth ribbon to the wayside cross, one for every year of the Mercy. He has walked his name clean off, and Whit says: *So have I.*
- **Fraying.** He frays a little more at each meeting.
- **His name.** Once the player has read his name at the hermit's hearth in Ninefold, Isot can read it to him at the Fair. He remembers the second walk of every road: once to sell, once to see how the ribbons were worn. He gives her his last ribbon, *Gervase's Ribbon* (a charm: the first time the wearer would fall in a battle, they stay at 1 HP), and asks a quarter less from then on. In the ending his name is read with the others.

## 7. Locations

Each location has its own palette (hex values from the pigment set in §8.5), a light and grade, a mood for its music, and at least one puzzle or secret. Each map opens with a location card (§10.5). The puzzles all build on one exploration verb, **raking light** (§10.3), so each new puzzle deepens a single idea instead of adding a new mechanic.

### 7.1 Saint Ebb's by night (Ch I)

- **Place.** A tidal island abbey: a scriptorium lined with chained books, the penitent's cell, a cloister and its roof, the church with Hild's anchorhold built into its wall, and the Sea Gate: the island and the abbey church above, the cliff and its stairs, the shore and the gate, and the long causeway running south into the mist.
- **Palette.** Lapis-deep `#172A5C`, lapis `#24418F`, azurite `#4A73B5`, slate `#5E6572`, lead-white `#F8F2E4`, gold `#C9A23C`. Vermilion `#C63D2A` appears only in rubrics.
- **Light.** The moon is the key light. Lamps, torches and candles make warm pools against the cool grade, stained glass glows, and the sea reflects the abbey.
- **Music.** D Dorian. Generative plainchant in organum (a second voice a fifth below), the tide breathing underneath, and a distant bell every forty seconds or so.
- **Environment.** Wystan's empty desk. Brother Cuthwin, who insists that desk has always been empty. The lectern motto. Bell-carts in the yard. The causeway knight whom the fishermen row around.
- **Puzzles and secrets.**
  - The raking light tutorial: the bricked-up doorway, then the painted-over door in the cloister.
  - **Secret:** Wystan's note in his psalter.
  - Lost Names 1–2.

### 7.2 Lychford, Midwinter (Ch II)

- **Place.** A fen village in snow: cottages, the green, the church and churchyard, a bell tower, the lych-gate covered in roses, and the frozen mere with the old ford beneath it.
- **Palette.** Minium `#DA6A32`, madder `#A63A4C`, brazil-rose `#D58193`, ochre `#BC8D42`, umber `#6C4B2D`, verdigris `#2E8B74`. Snow is lead-white shaded with azurite.
- **Light.** A low warm sun with long blue shadows; at night, lanterns round the green, then torches and the burning tower in the raid.
- **Music.** G Mixolydian in a lilting 6/8, on hurdy-gurdy drone and lute. The carol tune **never reaches its final note**; it loops just short of the cadence. After the bell puzzle, it finally resolves.
- **Environment.**
  - Ten-year-old roses.
  - No new graves.
  - The mirror-polished spade.
  - Fraying elders, and frayed pilgrims on the road to Varre.
  - The children's game of "Mercy".
  - The rehearsal of a play that can't end.
- **Puzzle: the passing bell.** The tower has four bells, and their names are rusted away until raking light shows them: *Morning*, *Weeper*, *Singer*, *Tenor*. The children's skipping rhyme gives the order:
  > *Weeper weeps and Morning calls,*
  > *Singer sings in empty halls;*
  > *wake the Tenor last of all,*
  > *and someone's name will heed the call.*

  The answer needs both sources, the rhyme heard in the village and the names read in the tower. Ringing in the wrong order just makes a clangour, with no penalty.
- **Secrets.**
  - Hild's empty grave (raking light on the headstone).
  - Lost Names 3–4.

### 7.3 The Blanchwood, Ninefold Blank and Knell Chapel (Ch III)

- **Place.** A forest that loses its colour and outline the deeper you go, grown over Ninefold Blank, a city drawn only in underwriting, with Ninefold Gate at its edge. The colour grade moves towards **grisaille**, the grey monochrome painting of Jean Pucelle, and then to bare vellum. At the centre stands Knell Chapel, Ninefold's chapel to the Reader, with an ossuary below.
- **Palette.** Sap green `#627F35`, terre-verte `#93A77F`, folium `#6B3C70`, umber `#6C4B2D`, bone `#E9DDC2`. Saturation drains with depth. The chapel uses bone, lamp-black `#18140F`, folium, silver `#B9BDC2` and a single vermilion.
- **Music.** E Phrygian, sparse, on solo recorder and psaltery. The generator **drops notes** more often the deeper you go, so the music frays too. At the chapel there is almost silence: wind and a very slow drum.
- **Puzzle: the Faded Path.** The road through Ninefold Blank has blanked out. Raking light shows the ghost of the old path, which you walk across visible nothing; its streets and doorways tell the story of the Scouring. A wrong step folds the wood back to the start of that stretch, with no damage.
- **Puzzle: the Danse Macabre mural.** Six panels, each a living figure led by a Death, have slid out of order. The inscription *"From the highest to the least the dance goes down"* gives the order: Pope, King, Knight, Merchant, Ploughman, Child. You restore it by swapping panels. The Knight's Death has no face, only a blank shield.
- **Secret.** A hermit's ruin with Lost Names 5–6.

### 7.4 The Margin (Ch IV)

- **Place.** The ornamental border of the world: giant acanthus and ivy scrolls, bars of burnished gold, drolleries everywhere, the Drollery Fair of the Court of Unreason, Wystan's vine and, at the bottom, the Ink-Well. The written page of Hollin hangs over it like a sky.
- **Palette.** Gold dominates (base `#C9A23C`, light `#F4DE8E`, dark `#85661E`), with vermilion `#C63D2A`, lapis `#24418F`, malachite `#4CAA6C`, brazil-rose `#D58193` and orpiment `#E2B73F`. The grade is saturated and glowing, and the ground itself is burnished gold. This is the most colourful place in the game, and the saddest.
- **Music.** F Lydian, bright and bouncy. Psaltery and recorder trade notes in **hocket** (a medieval technique where two instruments alternate single notes of one melody), over tabor and plucked vielle, slightly detuned so it feels comic and dreamlike. A low minor drone runs underneath. At Wystan's vine everything falls away to a solo psaltery playing the Book motif.
- **Puzzle: catchwords.** The Margin is made of the borders of thousands of pages. Each exit carries a scribe's catchword, the word written at the foot of a page that tells the binder which page comes next. The right sequence spells the Abbey motto Isot knows by heart: **WHAT · IS · WRITTEN · IS · HELD**. A wrong exit loops back to the start of that section.
- **Secrets.** Side branches hold Lost Names 7–8 and the optional fight. If Isot scraped Amabel, she can be found here.

### 7.5 Saint Ebb's at dawn (Ch V)

- **Place.** The same abbey as Chapter I, in new light: scriptorium, Abbot's lodging, cloister, and the church, whose nave floor blanks band by band under MERCY's tolls. The Fen Fyrd fights on the causeway below.
- **Palette.** Brazil-rose `#D58193`, gold-light `#F4DE8E`, azurite paling, lead-white. The Book itself is lamp-black and gold. **When FINIS is written, the full pigment set appears together for the first time.**
- **Light.** Dawn shafts through the rose window; a white ring of erasure draining colour as it passes.
- **Music.** The Chapter I chant returns with a second and third voice and morning birds, and a held tension note in the church. The ending is described in §9.3.
- **Secrets.**
  - The second half of the motto on the lectern.
  - Aumery's Book of Hours.
  - Lost Names 9–10.

## 8. Art direction

### 8.1 Medium and why

The game is **HD-2D**: pixel art standing in a lit 3D diorama, as in *Octopath Traveler*, with the proportions and readability of the 16-bit *Final Fantasy* and *Chrono Trigger* games. Light, shadow, fog, bloom and depth of field carry the mood, so a small sprite can stand in a scene that feels large and cold.

The manuscript has not gone. It lives in what the world is and in how the game speaks:

- **The world's rules are the Book's** (§1.3). Enemies are drolleries, forgetting is scraped vellum, and fraying drains a sprite's colour.
- **The colours are pigments** (§8.5). Every colour ramp starts from a historical pigment.
- **The page appears where the story is the Book:** the Great Page of the prologue, the interlude pages, the illuminated logo, the FINIS letters of the last battle, and the Margin, where the border of the page becomes a place.
- **The interface is rubricated:** lapis windows with gold borders, red for names and intents, a gold manicule for a cursor.

It's the right choice for three reasons:

1. **It reads at any size.** Small sprites and clear silhouettes stay legible on a phone, and the battle's places and intents have to be read at a glance.
2. **Light tells the story.** Candlelight in the scriptorium, the moon on the sea, the Blanchwood draining to grey and the dawn of Chapter V are all light and grade.
3. **It suits generation.** Pixel art made of ramps, dithering and outlines can be drawn by code at load, and the diorama turns those flat images into a place.

### 8.2 The diorama

**Camera and space.** The world is real 3D seen by a tilted orthographic camera, pitched 40° below the horizon. Maps are authored in art pixels as seen on screen (x right, y down the screen, h up from the ground), the way pixel artists draw them. The world is stretched so that every texel stays square on screen: depth is scaled by 1/sin of the pitch and height by 1/cos. There are 3 logical units per art pixel, so the 1280×720 view shows about 427×240 art pixels. Tiles are 16 art pixels.

**Terrain.** Each tile stands at its own height, in steps of 8 art pixels. Tops carry the painted ground. Where a tile stands above its neighbour, a face drops to it: rock for natural ground, masonry for paved platforms, with ambient occlusion shading the ground at its foot. Faces wear what lies on top: grass hangs over the lip and sits on the ledges, snow covers a winter bank with the stones showing through, and in the Margin the gold stands on its raised gesso. Stairs are built step by step, 4 steps (tread and riser) per tile, and the ground height slopes smoothly along them.

**Relief.** No map is a flat board. The ground banks up behind the paths, rising toward the back of the scene where the camera sees its faces, and buildings stand on terraces where the land does:
- the Sea Gate's island on its cliff;
- Lychford's sunken lane, the village's north terrace, the church a step above its graves, the far shore of the mere;
- the Blanchwood's banks;
- the gold of the Margin on its gesso, and the Edge where the page ends.

Behind every outdoor battle the ground rises in two steps. Banks bay in now and then rather than run ruler-straight. The places left level are level for a reason: rooms and the cloister garth, Ninefold Blank, which has been scraped flat, and the Ivy Road's catchword arches. A test walks each map from its entrances, climbing no more than a figure can, and checks that everyone and everything is still within reach.

**Buildings** are textured volumes. Walls are boxes whose south face carries a painted elevation (coursed stone, doors, stained glass that glows at night). Buttresses stand out with set-offs and cast shadows. Roofs are gables and lean-tos of slate, towers end in spires, and walls are crowned with merlons. You can walk through an arch.

**Sprites.** Figures, trees and props stand as upright billboards. Their normals lean back towards the sky, so they catch light from above as well as in front, and they cast shadows shaped like their silhouettes. A figure standing in water has its legs hidden under the surface.

**Life in the scene.**
- **Ground clutter** (grass tufts, flowers, pebbles, marram) stands in its hundreds as instanced meshes, one draw call per kind.
- **Particles** are 3D and additive: fireflies, embers off torches, motes, snow, sparks and glints on water. Bloom turns them into small lights.
- **Light shafts** are soft beams falling from windows, spreading under lanterns and slanting through mist, with dust drifting in them.
- **Water** is a plane whose shader works on the art-pixel grid: depth bands, ripples, foam breathing along the shore and the moon's broken path. It mixes in a planar reflection of the scene that wobbles row by row, like reflections in pixel art.
- **The sky** is a backdrop at the far edge of the map, drawn on the art-pixel grid: a banded gradient, twinkling stars, thin clouds lit by the moon, the moon and its halo. It shows above the horizon and in the water.

### 8.3 Light, post-processing and quality

**Lighting.** A hemisphere light gives the sky's ambient. The moon (or the sun) is the directional key light, with soft VSM shadows. A pool of point lights is reassigned each frame to the lamps, torches and candles nearest the centre of the view, favouring the larger and brighter. Stained glass, lamp glass and flames carry emissive textures, so they glow and bloom.

**Post chain.** The scene renders into an HDR target, with MSAA where the tier allows and a depth texture. Then, in order:

1. Depth of field from the real depth buffer: a sharp band around the subject, ramping to full blur.
2. Distance fog, and low mist that drifts as noise.
3. Bloom through a mip chain of five levels.
4. Colour grade: exposure, contrast, saturation, lift and gain, per location.
5. Vignette and grain.
6. Fades and flashes for cinematics; flashes are scaled down by the accessibility setting.

Every pass that reads the scene drops a pixel that is not a finite number and holds the rest under a ceiling. Without that, one bad pixel (a GPU's NaN from `pow()` of a base a hair below zero, or an overflow of the half-float target) spreads through the bloom and depth-of-field levels into a flat pale rectangle. The test is on the float's bits, which shader compilers cannot optimise away.

Each location supplies an *atmosphere* (sky and ground ambient, key light colour and direction, fog, mist) and a *grade*.

**Quality tiers.** The tier is picked from the device: phones (a coarse pointer, or a short side under 600 px) get low, or medium with at least 6 GB of memory and 8 cores; desktops get high, or medium with less than 4 GB or 4 cores. `?quality=low|medium|high` forces a tier, and so does the Quality setting.

| | Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Device pixel ratio cap | 1 | 1.5 | 2 |
| MSAA | off | off | 4× |
| Shadow map | 512 | 1024 | 2048 |
| Point lights | 4 | 8 | 12 |
| Water reflections | off | 35% of screen resolution | 50% |
| Bloom | on | on | on |
| Depth of field | off | on | on |
| Particle density | 40% | 70% | 100% |

**Adaptive render scale.** The render scale follows the frame time: it drops by 10% when frames stay slow (over 24 ms on average), and climbs back by 5% after a long run of fast frames (under 13 ms), never below 55%. The Resolution setting can fix it instead (§10.8).

### 8.4 Pixel art

- **The toolkit.** Every image is a small RGBA buffer drawn with crisp primitives (no anti-aliasing), coloured from hue-shifted ramps, dithered with a Bayer matrix and finished with selective outlines. Light comes from the upper left. Things that glow get a second, emissive image.
- **Characters** are 32×48 frames, about four heads tall: small faces, sloping shoulders, cloth that falls in folds and swings with the stride, sleeves that widen at the wrist, and a rim of light along the lit edge. Each has four directions (right mirrors left), a four-frame breathing idle and a six-frame walk. They are built from data-driven specs (skin, hair, eyes, headwear, robe, belt, cape, armour, shield, the item held, plague-marks).
- **Portraits** are 48×48 busts drawn from the same specs, lit from the upper left, with a catch-light in the eyes. Brows, lids and mouth change with eight moods: neutral, warm, wry, sad, grave, stern, alarmed and tired.
- **Drolleries** each get their own generator, so the silhouettes stay distinct: gryllus, snail, hare, babewyn, wodewose, caladrius, bishop-fish, skeleton dancers and the ape-scribe. The Blot is a shader (metaball-style shapes with feathered ink edges), not a sprite.
- **Architecture** is drawn in the 3/4 view of the 16-bit RPGs: coursed stone, slate seen from above, buttresses that step back as they rise, lancet windows, doors, a bell tower with a spire.
- **Nature.** Canopies are clusters of leafy blobs, each shaded as a little sphere and broken into leaf clumps by noise, so they read as foliage rather than balls. Bushes, rocks and reeds follow the same rules.
- **Ground** is painted pixel by pixel from the map's layout: soft terrains meet along noisy edges, raised terrain casts a lip of shadow, cliffs get strata and grass overhang, and water is left clear for the animated water beneath, with a distance-to-shore map for its foam.
- **Props:** lanterns and torches with emissive glass and animated flames, boats, posts, crates, barrels, crosses and gravestones.

### 8.6 Shape language and fraying

| What | Shapes |
|---|---|
| **People** | About four heads tall, with small faces and cloth that falls in long folds. Clear silhouettes: Isot's coif and inkhorn, Hild's wimple and chained psalter, Whit's great helm and lance. |
| **Marginalia (enemies)** | Round, absurd and hybrid. Bulbous bodies, tails that curl into vines, silly faces. Comic-grotesque, never gory. |
| **The Abbey and Aumery** | Verticals, pointed arches, strict symmetry and straight lines: order and control. |
| **Lychford** | Low, round and lumpy. Thatch, snow drifts and crooked timber. |
| **The Margin** | Spirals, acanthus and ivy leaves, endless curling scrollwork: wild growth. |
| **Death and Whit** | Blank argent: an absence. Clean, unadorned and calm. |

**Fraying** is the painter's work run backwards. A sprite drains down its colour ramps to grey, its outline breaks, and then it dissolves pixel by pixel to blank. A single `fray` value from 0 to 1 drives this on any sprite or patch of ground, and the Blanchwood also grades the whole scene towards grisaille. Defeated enemies fray to outlines and scatter into the Margin. **Colour returning**, in the ending, is the same process run forwards.

### 8.7 Animation

- **Figures** animate in frames: the breathing idle and the walk, with emotes over their heads. Isot leads; the others follow her path.
- **In battle:** a lunge for an attack, with an ink-splash where the hit lands and red damage numbers; a shake for a hurt; the figure tips over when it falls.
- **The world moves on its own:** flames flicker, lamps sway their light, the sea breathes along the shore, mist drifts, and fireflies and embers rise.

### 8.8 Typography and the look of the interface

- **Text** uses system serif fonts only: `"Palatino Linotype", "Book Antiqua", Palatino, "Iowan Old Style", Georgia, "Times New Roman", serif`. The interface is drawn crisp at full resolution over the pixel world, never pixelated.
- **Windows** are lapis gradients with a gold double border and corner flourishes. The cursor is a gold **manicule** (☞, the pointing hand drawn in real manuscript margins).
- **Rubrication.** Speaker names, key terms and enemy intents are in vermilion.
- **The logo** is an illuminated initial P in a lapis square with gold vines, then the rest of the name in gold capitals, with the motto beneath (*What is written is held*). It inks itself in from left to right, and a glint runs across the gold now and then.

## 9. Audio direction

Everything is synthesised at runtime with the Web Audio API. There are no samples.

### 9.1 Instruments

| Instrument | How it's made |
|---|---|
| Psaltery, lute, harp | Plucked strings (Karplus–Strong) |
| Vielle, hurdy-gurdy | Bowed saw waves through a low-pass filter, with vibrato. The hurdy-gurdy adds a "buzz" from waveshaping. |
| Chant voice | Saw waves through vowel formants, layered in organum (parallel fifths and fourths) |
| Recorder | Sine plus breath noise |
| Tabor | A pitched membrane plus noise |
| Bells | FM and additive synthesis with inharmonic partials. Bells are central: the Abbey bells, the passing bell, MERCY and the FINIS tolls. |
| Organ drone | Additive sine waves |

### 9.2 Composition

Music is written in the **medieval church modes**. Every location has a short composed motif stored in data, which the generator develops with chant-like melodic contours, cadences on the mode's home note, and small variations, so it sounds alive without being random noise. Generated chant moves mostly by step inside the mode, starts on the final or the fifth, and cadences on the final.

**Leitmotifs**

| Motif | Description | Where it returns |
|---|---|---|
| **The Book** | D Dorian chant: D F G A G F E D | The battle theme and the final boss |
| **Hild's lullaby** | A short falling phrase, first heard faintly through the squint | The wall coming down, the nave (inverted), the ending |
| **The Knell** | A low bell, a fifth and a falling fourth | Whit's memories, the bell puzzle and the FINIS tolls |
| **Isot's flourish** | A quick rising figure, like a pen stroke | Dialogue openings and victories |

### 9.3 Music by location

| Location | Mode / tempo | Instruments | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saint Ebb's, night | D Dorian, free rhythm | Chant voice in organum, organ drone, tide noise, distant bell | Hushed, cold and holy. The causeway adds gulls and a fog drone. |
| Lychford | G Mixolydian, 6/8 at about 92 bpm | Hurdy-gurdy, lute | The tune **can't cadence** until the bell rings, and then it resolves. |
| Blanchwood | E Phrygian, about 60 bpm | Recorder, psaltery | **Notes drop out** with depth. Near silence at the chapel; low organ and bone clicks in the ossuary. |
| The Margin | F Lydian, about 112 bpm | Psaltery and recorder in hocket, tabor, plucked vielle | Comic, detuned and dreamlike over a low minor drone. MERCY's toll is out of tune with it. |
| Saint Ebb's, dawn | D Hypodorian | Chant in three voices, birds | Tension in the church. |
| Interludes | — | Solo psaltery and a quiet chant voice | Turning pages. |

**Battle theme: "Estampie".** D Dorian in 6/8 with a brisk dotted pulse, in the shape of a medieval dance. A frame drum and tabor keep the pulse, a psaltery plucks a two-bar ostinato over a drone on D and A, and a shawm plays the tune's puncta with open and closed endings, then rests for four bars while the drum carries on.

- **Boss variant:** heavier, at about 126 bpm, with low bells on the downbeats and the melody doubled a fifth below.
- **Final boss:** the Book motif in long notes over the estampie. Each Inscribed letter adds a bell, and after S the music cuts to silence, followed by the Lychford passing bell.

**Ending.** Hild's lullaby on psaltery, then the full consort. The last cadence is the only **major chord** in the whole score (a "Picardy third"), saved for the moment the world can finally end.

|---|---|

| Hits | Ink splat: a low thump plus wet noise |

| Hild's walls | Stone crack and grind |

### 9.5 Mixing

- Buses for music, ambience, effects, voices and UI feed a master compressor. The settings set master, music, ambience, effects and voices; the UI follows the effects level.
- Reverb uses convolution with **impulse responses generated at runtime**: a long one for the church, a medium one for the cloister, a dry one outdoors, and a shimmering one in the Margin.
- Audio starts only after the first key press, click or touch, to satisfy browser autoplay rules. M mutes.

## 10. Exploration and interface

### 10.1 Movement

- Isot leads, and the others follow in a short line along her path.
- Movement is free and smooth, with collision against a tile grid and round blockers for props and people.
- Click or tap the ground to walk there; the path is found with A*.
- The camera follows softly and is bounded by the map.

### 10.2 Interaction

- A small red manicule, the Glossators' chalk (§1.6), marks anything interactive: people, objects, doors, lecterns and encounters.
- Enemies on the map are drawn in place, idling, with a small red banderole showing their name. Touching them starts the fight.

### 10.3 Raking light (the exploration verb)

Hold the raking-light action (R, the right mouse button, or Y on a gamepad) and Isot tilts her candle. A soft circle around her reveals the **underwriting**: the scraped text and lines left in the vellum of the world. It reveals:

- hidden doors and paths,
- scraped inscriptions,
- puzzle clues,
- Lost Names.

It is taught in the first minute and it drives every puzzle, which keeps the systems small and on-theme.

### 10.4 Dialogue

- A lapis window along the bottom of the screen, with the speaker's 48×48 portrait in its mood, a name plate in red, and text that types itself out with the speaker's voice. A blinking manicule means "continue".
- Choices open a small window above it, with the gold manicule.
- Narration has no portrait. Text speed (slow, normal, fast, instant) and larger text are settings.

### 10.5 Cards, letterbox, subtitles and interludes

- **Location cards:** the place's name fades in over the scene between two gold rules, with a line beneath (*Saint Ebb's — The Sea Gate, by night*). Chapter cards use the same form (*Chapter I · The Scraping*).
- **Letterbox bars** close in for cinematics.
- **Subtitles** carry narration over cinematics, as in the prologue.
- **Interludes** are full illuminated pages of Isot's chronicle: a large decorated initial containing a scene, a red title, three to five lines of her prose and a vine border. Each ends with a page turn.

### 10.6 Title screen

The abbey of Saint Ebb's at night, seen by a camera that drifts slowly along the island, with the lamps lit and the sea moving below. The logo (§8.8) inks itself in and glints. Then **Press any key** (on touch screens, **Touch to begin**); this first press also starts the audio. A short column of choices appears under the logo, each lit by the gold manicule when chosen:

- **New game:** the prologue (§3.3).
- **Continue:** the latest save, autosave or manual (§12). Once the story is finished, it reads **The last page** instead and opens the epilogue page again, as a keepsake.
- **Settings** (§10.8).
- **Credits.**

### 10.7 Pause menu

Escape, Start or the touch Menu button opens it, anywhere except the title screen and the prologue. A column of entries on the left, the chosen page on the right:

- **Resume.**
- **Party:** each member's portrait, role, HP and abilities with their costs, and the battle **formation**: choose two of the Front, Middle and Rear to swap who stands there when a battle begins.
- **Equipment:** each member's relic and charm slots, and the items carried (§6).
- **Settings** (§10.8).
- **Save:** writes the manual slot (§12).
- **Return to title**, after a confirmation that unsaved progress will be lost.

- **Journal** (also J, or Select on a gamepad): the chapter, what to do next in Isot's words (worked out from the map and the story so far), the Lost Names found out of ten, and the time played.

The windows are lapis with a gold double border and a gold manicule cursor (§8.8). The menu works the same with every input: up and down move the manicule, left and right change a value, confirm chooses, cancel goes back; mouse and touch pick rows directly. While it is open, the world pauses.

### 10.8 Settings

Settings are kept apart from game saves (§12) and apply at once.

| Mute | M | — | — | — |

**Hints name one device.** The story's own words never name a key. When the player must be told how to do something, a small hint window low on the screen says it for the device in hand: *hold R* at the keyboard (or whatever R was rebound to), *hold Y* with a gamepad, *hold the candle button* on a touch screen. It follows the last device used, changes as soon as the player switches, and goes once the control is used. A touch screen is assumed from the start where the pointer is coarse, and the Hints setting can fix one device. The same applies to the help line under a page, the title's *press any key* and the battle's number keys (shown at the keyboard only).

The touch controls appear on the first touch and stay out of the way of a mouse. The virtual stick appears wherever the left thumb lands; A and B sit under the right thumb, and Menu in the corner. Taps anywhere else advance dialogue or walk to the spot.

**Battle** (the screen is in progress; §14). A root window asks *who acts?* and lists the allies who still can, then **Step**, **Undo** and **End turn**. Choosing an ally opens their abilities (1–5 pick one directly); an ability then asks for its target: an enemy, an ally, or an intent on a banderole. **First-time tips.** The first time something appears on the field, a short tip, written on a slip of vellum like the banderoles with its subject as a red rubric, says what it is in a line or two while the hand points at it, once in a playthrough: the banderoles (order, damage), the marks over the heads, the turn (each ally acts once; *End turn*, *Undo*), hidden intents and the Gloss, wind-ups, Ink (from round 2: what Strike Through costs, how much Isot holds, how much comes back, so the player sees it cannot be spent every round), places and *Step*, and Strike Through against Emend with their costs. The words are the game's, short and concrete; no tip explains what another already has. The battle waits until it is read; any press or tap goes on. Each tip names the controls of the device in hand, and is remembered in the save. The help line at the top says only what the screen does not already show. Over an ally it is empty unless they bear a status, and then it says what each one means (*Ward 3: absorbs the next 3 damage*); it never repeats a name or HP. Over a target it says what that target's statuses mean, or else what the ability will do; over a banderole, the intent in words; over a refused choice, why. Confirm chooses, cancel goes back a level, and mouse and touch pick allies, abilities, targets and banderoles directly.

**Debug:** `` ` `` (the ² key on AZERTY) toggles the overlay; Shift + `` ` `` is kept for the debug menu (§13.6).

## 11. Platforms and languages

### 11.1 Platforms

- **Desktop and mobile browsers.** The game draws into a 16:9 area of 1280×720 logical units, letterboxed inside the window, and the quality tier and render scale adapt to the device (§8.3).
- **Landscape.** On a phone held upright, an overlay shows a phone turning and asks the player to turn the device sideways.
- **Installable.** The build is a Progressive Web App: a web manifest, a service worker that precaches every file of the build so the game plays offline, and app icons drawn by code at build time. Installed, it runs full screen in landscape.
- **The touch buttons stand beside the game.** On a phone wider than the game's 16:9 (most of them), the black band beside it holds the buttons in a column (the menu at the top, B at the foot, A over it, the candle over A), smaller if they must be but never below a thumb's size, so that nothing covers the field. Where there is no band they sit over the corner, and the battle's banderoles keep clear of them.
- **Full screen, never zoomed.** On a touch screen the first touch puts the game in full screen and locks it sideways where the browser allows it (Android, iPad), and a later touch puts it back if the player left it; the Full screen setting turns this off, and at a desk enters or leaves it. An iPhone's Safari cannot put a page in full screen, so there the title says, once, how to add the game to the home screen, from which it opens full screen. The page never zooms: pinches and double taps on the game are refused (iOS ignores the viewport's own refusal), and the canvas always matches the visible screen, not the taller 100vh of a phone with its toolbars.

### 11.2 Languages

The game ships in **English and French**. The language is detected from the browser's preferred languages (`navigator.languages`; the first one the game speaks, or English), and the player can switch it live in the settings: every window, card and line changes at once.

- **Every player-facing string is bilingual.** Interface strings are keyed tables, one per language, with named placeholders. Story lines and data (abilities, items, enemy names, intents, cards, subtitles) carry their text as `{ en, fr }` objects next to the content.
- **Text drawn into images**, such as the logo's motto, is redrawn when the language changes.
- **Terms.** The French follows the game's own vocabulary. An ability's name is a plain word the player already knows, and its scribe's sense comes from the story: Isot *raye* and *corrige* as a copyist does (*ne gratte jamais, Isot. ## 12. Saving and loading

- **Storage:** `localStorage`, one key per slot (`palimpsest:save:v1:auto` and `palimpsest:save:v1:manual`). Each save has a version number, with migration functions when the format changes. Version 2 added the items found at story beats: a version 1 save is given those whose beats it has already passed.
- **Slots:** one autosave and one manual save. **Continue** on the title screen loads whichever is more recent.
- **Manual saves** come from the pause menu's Save, anywhere the pause menu opens.
- **Autosave checkpoints:** entering any map, after every battle, at every chapter start, and after key story beats.
- **Contents:**
  - version, slot and timestamp
  - chapter, map, spawn point, position and facing
  - story flags (choices are flags)
  - the party and its formation
  - unlocked abilities
  - equipment worn and items carried
  - cleared encounters
  - Lost Names found
  - play time
- **Settings** live under their own key (`palimpsest:settings:v1`), not in saves. They are validated field by field: a missing or damaged field falls back to its default, so an old or broken file never stops the game. Where storage is unavailable (private browsing, a full disk), settings still apply for the session.
- **Robustness:** a corrupt or unknown save is ignored with a notice, never a crash. A chapter-select debug option can create a valid save state for any point in the game.

The battle engine, story runner, saves, settings, input mapping, quality picking and diorama space are pure TypeScript, tested without a browser.

### 13.3 Content as typed data

All content is typed TypeScript, checked at compile time and easy to tweak.

- **Enemies** are a name, HP and a `behave` function that returns the round's intents from the enemy's own pattern counter, its HP and its side:

```ts
// battle/data.ts
wodewose: {
  name: { en: 'Wodewose', fr: 'Homme sauvage' },
  hp: 16,
  behave: (c) =>
    c.phase % 2 === 0
      ? [{ label: { en: 'Gathers itself to club the Front · 9', fr: 'Se ramasse pour assommer l’Avant · 9' },
           target: { place: 0 }, damage: 9, reach: 'close', countdown: 1 }]
      : [{ label: { en: 'Roars: the party’s Ward is stripped', fr: 'Rugit : la Garde du groupe tombe' },
           target: { all: true }, effects: [{ kind: 'stripWard' }], reach: 'any' }],
},
```

- **Encounters** name the enemies, the party, the stage, and an optional environment intent per round (the tide).
- **Maps** are an ASCII ground layout with a matching grid of height digits, plus props, people, triggers, exits, underwriting, encounter markers, switches (tiles that open when a flag is set) and spawn points, each with optional conditions on story flags.
- **Scripts** are lists of steps: say (with a mood), narrate, choice, label, goto, if, set, join, leave, unlock, battle, map, move, face, show, hide, card, wait, sfx, emit, autosave, lostName, chapter and end. The runner asks a host to do each one: the map scene in the game, a fake in tests. Cinematics add the director's camera and screen steps (§4.1).

### 13.4 Battle engine

The battle engine is pure, deterministic and fully unit-tested. The screen drives it and animates what it reports; it never changes the state itself.

```ts
const b = new Battle({ encounter: 'b1', party: ['whit', 'hild', 'isot'], abilities, equipment, seed, level, difficulty });
b.start();                                    // round 1: the Omen
b.check('isot', 'strike', { intent: 'i2' });  // a refusal ('ink', 'from-front', 'reach', 'too-strong', ...) or null
b.act('isot', 'strike', { intent: 'i2' });    // resolves at once; targets are { unit }, { intent } or, for Emend, { intent, to }
b.step(0, 1);                                 // the free Step between two neighbouring places
b.undo();                                     // takes back the last action or Step of this phase
b.endTurn();                                  // enemy phase, round's end, next Omen
b.aims(intent);                               // the allies an intent would strike as things stand
b.events;                                     // append-only log: omen, act, damage, fizzle, cancel, move, reckoning, fall...
b.result;                                     // 'ongoing' | 'victory' | 'defeat'
```

- `party` lists the formation front to back. Equipment is applied by the engine (§6).
- Undo restores snapshots taken during the party phase and truncates the event log to match.
- Refusals: the battle is over, no such ally, fallen, already acted, immured, unknown ability, already used, must stand Front or Middle, not enough Ink, not enough HP, bad target, target immured, out of reach, too strong to Read, not a single blow.

**Tests** cover:

- every ability and status, and how they interact (Ward expiry, Tally to Reckoning, Immure delay, Shelled caps, Emend retargeting, carry-over rules for empty or fallen places, reach and fizzles, wind-ups, intents declared at the Omen);
- every item;
- every boss script and its intended solutions;
- every fight at the level the story gives, in each difficulty (plain fighting, and the answer a beam search finds);
- levels, ranks and the spoils of a fight, given once;
- the victory and defeat conditions;
- determinism under a seed;
- the story script runner (flags, branching);
- save and settings parsing and migration.

### 13.5 Performance

- The target is 60 fps on integrated GPUs and recent phones, held by the quality tiers and the adaptive render scale (§8.3).
- Pixel art is generated at load per location and disposed of when you leave. Ground clutter is instanced, point lights are pooled, and shadow maps are drawn once per frame, not again for the reflection.

### 13.6 Debug tools

These ship in every build, including the GitHub Pages one, because you'll be testing there.

- **Overlay** (`` ` ``, or `?debug` in the URL): fps and frame time, scene and location, the quality tier, toggles for each rendering effect (shadows, reflections, bloom, depth of field, fog, grade) and the scene's own buttons (such as skipping an opening).
- **Scene links:** `?scene=prologue`, `?scene=seagate`, `?quality=low|medium|high`.
- **Debug menu** (Shift + `` ` ``), with its milestone (§14):
  - jump to the start of any chapter, or into any of the 14 fights, with the correct unlocks and items for that point (`?chapter=3`, `?fight=B3`);
  - chapter, map position, story flags and the random seed;
  - battle state: round, phase, Ink, each unit's HP, statuses and intent;
  - set and clear flags, unlock all abilities and items, win the current battle, god mode, always-on raking light, audio mute, clear save.

### 13.7 CI/CD

`.github/workflows/ci.yml` runs the tests and the build on every pull request. `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` runs on every push to `main`: install, run the tests, build, and deploy `dist/` to GitHub Pages using `actions/upload-pages-artifact` and `actions/deploy-pages`. The repo's Pages source must be set to **GitHub Actions** once, in Settings → Pages.

## 15. Scope guardrails

**Out of scope by design:**

- random encounters, and anything that can be farmed (every fight is fought once; every reward is placed)
- crafting
- random loot
- voice acting (dialogue uses voice blips)
- languages beyond English and French

**Cut first if quality slips, in this order:**

1. The optional fight F8.
2. The Tide in Boss I.
3. The mural puzzle, which would become a simple reveal.
4. The catchword puzzle, which would become a single riddle gate.

The story beats, the five bosses, raking light and the fray effect are the core and won't be cut.

## 16. Questions for review

1. **Title.** Is *Palimpsest* fine, or would you like a plainer name?
2. **Darkness.** The story touches on mercy-killing. Isot scrapes her suffering teacher at his request, and Amabel asks the same of her. It is handled with restraint, with no gore and with consequences. Is that tone OK?
3. **Final battle.** Boss V is an objective fight (protect the scribe for five letters) rather than a race to empty an HP bar. Do you agree?
